# Lesson 14: Weather Hazards — Thunderstorms, Icing, and Cockpit Weather Limitations
## PPL_PA_I_C_05 | ACS Elements: PA.I.C.K3, PA.I.C.R1, PA.I.C.R2

### What This Lesson Covers
Task C closes with the hazards themselves: thunderstorms and microbursts, structural vs. carburetor icing, fog types, and the hard truth about what your FIS-B radar can and cannot do. These four topics produce the most frequently fatal general aviation accidents in the NTSB database.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: Thunderstorm — Three Ingredients, Three Stages, Microburst Trap**
Ingredients: Moisture + Unstable Lapse Rate + Lifting Force. Stages: Cumulus (updrafts grow) → Mature (most intense — rain at surface, updrafts AND downdrafts, hail, lightning) → Dissipating (downdrafts dominate).

Microburst: less than 2.5nm diameter, less than 1,000 ft deep. Performance trap: headwind (performance improves, pilot pitches up) → central downdraft up to 6,000 fpm → tailwind (lift lost). The pilot who pitches up into the downdraft phase will stall near the ground. Do not attempt takeoff or landing when microburst suspected. Divert.

**RKP_02: Structural vs. Carburetor Icing**
Structural icing: visible moisture + temperature at or below 0°C at the wing surface. Clear ice = worst type (large drops spread rearward, heavy, streamlined). Rime ice = small drops freeze instantly on leading edge (rough, white opaque). Frost: no ice shape change but destroys boundary layer — remove entirely before takeoff.

Carburetor icing: DOES NOT require visible moisture or freezing air temp. Can form at +90°F (32°C) with humidity above 60%. The carb venturi creates a refrigeration effect. Symptoms: RPM drop, rough engine. Remedy: FULL carb heat (not partial — partial heat melts ice into water that re-freezes).

**RKP_03: Fog Types — The Four-Item Checkride Menu**
Radiation fog: clear night + calm winds + high humidity → surface cools to dewpoint. Key: CALM winds.
Advection fog: warm moist air moves over cold surface. Key: requires WIND (horizontal transport).
Upslope fog: air forced up terrain cools adiabatically to dewpoint. Key: TERRAIN.
Steam fog: cold air over warm water. Key: WARM WATER + COLD AIR.

The DPE will describe conditions. Name the type from clues: calm = radiation, wind = advection, terrain = upslope, warm lake/river = steam.

**RKP_04: Cockpit Weather Limitations — AC 00-63B**
FIS-B NEXRAD lag: 15–20 minutes from atmospheric scan to cockpit screen. Use it for: STRATEGIC routing decisions 50+ miles from weather. Do NOT use it for: TACTICAL threading between storm cells. A clear gap on your iPad may already be a storm at FL350.

FIS-B is line-of-sight (978 MHz UAT). Low altitude + terrain = no signal. Exactly when you need weather data most, you may lose it.
