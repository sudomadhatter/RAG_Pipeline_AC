# Lesson 12: TAFs, GFA, and Winds Aloft Forecasts
## PPL_PA_I_C_03 | ACS Elements: PA.I.C.K2b, PA.I.C.K2c, PA.I.C.K2e

### What This Lesson Covers
The three forecast products you will use on every cross-country: the TAF (only covers 5SM from the airport), the GFA (covers everything in between), and the Winds Aloft forecast (True North, 9900 = calm, altitude exclusion rules). Plus the TAF change group codes that tell you when conditions are changing.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: TAF — Five Saint Miles Only**
The TAF is a forecast for one airport's terminal area only. The hard geographic limit is a 5-statute-mile radius from the center of the runway complex. At mile 6, the TAF is meteorologically meaningless. A pilot who uses the destination TAF to evaluate the en route airspace is making a potentially fatal assumption. The TAF + TAF = Bookend Planning without an en route product is an incomplete briefing.

**RKP_02: GFA — The FA Replacement**
The Graphical Forecasts for Aviation (GFA) replaced the legacy text Area Forecast in 2017. Do not call it 'the area forecast' on a checkride. The GFA lives at AviationWeather.gov and shows: cloud coverage, flight category, precipitation, icing, turbulence, winds, temperatures — all in interactive map layers. It covers 14 hours past, 15 hours forward in 3-hour steps. It is your primary tool for evaluating what the 90 miles of airspace between two airports looks like.

**RKP_03: Winds Aloft (FB) — Three Rules**
Rule 1: Wind direction is True North (same nuance as METAR text — convert to magnetic for heading).
Rule 2: Code 9900 = calm/light variable winds, less than 5 knots.
Rule 3a: Wind direction/speed are NOT forecast within 1,500 feet of station elevation.
Rule 3b: Temperature is NOT forecast within 2,500 feet of station elevation, and the 3,000-foot level carries no temperature data at all (temperatures start at 6,000 MSL).

High wind code: if direction digits exceed 36, subtract 50 from direction and add 100 to speed.

**RKP_04: TAF Change Groups — FM, BECMG, TEMPO, PROB**
FM (From): immediate complete change at the stated time — everything before FM does not apply after that time.
BECMG (Becoming): gradual change between two times; the endpoint defines when the new conditions are fully in effect.
TEMPO (Temporary): fluctuating, each occurrence less than 1 hour, less than half the forecast period. Expect it to pop up and go away.
PROB30/PROB40: 30–40% or 40–50% probability. Treat PROB40 TEMPO thunderstorms in your arrival window as operationally significant.
