# Lesson: Privileges & Limitations
## ACS Reference: Area I, Task A — PPL_PA_I_A_01

This is a ground school briefing for PPL student pilots preparing for their oral exam.
The examiner (DPE) will expect the student to demonstrate understanding of these topics.

---

## Required Knowledge Point 1: Certification & Currency

**Why this matters:** Ensures pilots meet baseline legal and safety standards before carrying passengers.

**What the student must know:**
A private pilot must hold a valid pilot certificate, a current medical or BasicMed, and a government-issued photo ID. To carry passengers, pilots need 3 takeoffs and landings within the preceding 90 days in the same category, class, and type (full stop at night or in tailwheel aircraft). A flight review is required every 24 calendar months.

**FAR Reference:** 14 CFR 61.56, 14 CFR 61.57, 14 CFR 61.51, 14 CFR 61.103, 14 CFR 1.1

---

## Required Knowledge Point 2: Privileges & Limitations

**Why this matters:** Prevents private pilots from illegally competing with commercial operators and assuming inappropriate risk.

**What the student must know:**
Private pilots may not carry passengers or property for compensation or hire. They may share operating expenses (fuel, oil, rental fees) with passengers on a pro rata basis, provided there is a bona fide common purpose for the flight. Exceptions include incidental business flying, providing no passengers are carried, and charitable airlifts under strict sponsor and experience limits.

**FAR Reference:** 14 CFR 61.113, 14 CFR 91.146, 14 CFR 61.69

---

## Required Knowledge Point 3: Proficiency vs. Currency

**Why this matters:** Legal currency alone does not guarantee the pilot possesses the skills needed for safe flight in challenging conditions.

**What the student must know:**
Currency is complying with the legal minimum to operate, such as logging 3 landings in 90 days. Proficiency is the actual competence to safely execute a flight and handle emergencies, which degrades over time independently of legal status. Pilots must actively employ personal minimums and honestly evaluate their recent experience using risk paradigms like PAVE.

**FAR Reference:** FAA-H-8083-2A, FAA P-8740-36
