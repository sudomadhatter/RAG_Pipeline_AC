# Lesson 7: Airworthiness Directives and SAIBs
## PPL_PA_I_B_03 | ACS Element: PA.I.B.K1c

### What This Lesson Covers
Airworthiness Directives are federal law. This lesson covers their legal structure, who bears responsibility for compliance, why manufacturer Service Bulletins are NOT legally binding under Part 91, and how to audit AD compliance in a logbook during a checkride oral.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: ADs Are Amendments to 14 CFR Part 39**
An Airworthiness Directive is a formal amendment to 14 CFR Part 39. It has the full force of federal law. Violating an AD is not a manufacturer recommendation failure — it is a federal regulatory violation. When the FAA identifies an unsafe condition that likely exists in multiple aircraft of the same type design, they issue an AD to mandate correction.

Two issuance pathways:
- Emergency AD: immediate effective date, no comment period, distributed urgently to all registered owners.
- Standard AD: published as a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking in the Federal Register, public comment period, then issued as a Final Rule.

Two compliance categories:
- One-time AD: perform once, log it, done. No further tracking required.
- Recurring AD: must be performed at specified intervals (e.g., inspect the fuel selector every 500 hours). These require active tracking during every logbook audit.

**RKP_02: Who Is Responsible for AD Compliance?**
14 CFR 91.403(a) places PRIMARY responsibility for AD compliance squarely on the registered owner or operator. Not the mechanic. Not the flight school. The owner. This is an active administrative obligation — the owner must proactively ensure recurring ADs are tracked and completed on time.

The PIC has a separate, parallel obligation under 14 CFR 91.7: if you dispatch an aircraft with an overdue recurring AD, you are in violation of 91.7 even if you are renting and had no idea. This dual enforcement exposure (owner under 91.403, PIC under 91.7) is what makes AD knowledge non-optional for any pilot acting as PIC.

AMOC: Alternative Method of Compliance. If the AD-prescribed repair method is unavailable (parts no longer made, facility not accessible), the owner can propose an AMOC to the FAA. It must be formally approved before use — it is not a self-declared workaround.

**RKP_03: SAIBs and Service Bulletins — Recommendations, Not Law**
Special Airworthiness Information Bulletins are governed by FAA Order 8110.100. They are non-regulatory alerts — the FAA equivalent of a yellow caution flag. Compliance is completely voluntary under Part 91.

Manufacturer Service Bulletins are engineering recommendations. Even if the manufacturer stamps 'MANDATORY' on the front page in red, that word carries no legal weight under Part 91. Only the FAA can impose mandatory maintenance through an AD. A private owner under Part 91 can legally choose not to comply with an SB — but if that SB is subsequently referenced in an AD, compliance becomes mandatory immediately.

The direction of escalation always goes: SAIB → possible SB adoption → possible FAA AD. Track SAIBs as early warnings of future ADs.

**RKP_04: Performing an AD Compliance Audit**
During the checkride, you may be given aircraft logbooks and asked to verify AD compliance. Locate the AD compliance record section. For each recurring AD: find the AD number, the interval (hours or calendar), the last compliance entry (hours/date), and compare against the current total time. If the last compliance entry plus the interval puts you past the current time, the AD is overdue. Aircraft is unairworthy.

The FAA CAROL database (Compliance and Airworthiness Records Online Library) is the authoritative searchable database for all active ADs. Know it exists and know how to use it.

### Key Regulations
- 14 CFR Part 39 (Airworthiness Directives — the legal authority)
- 14 CFR 91.403(a) (Owner/operator primary responsibility)
- 14 CFR 91.7 (PIC responsibility for airworthy condition)
- 14 CFR 91.417 (Maintenance record retention)
- FAA Order 8110.100 (SAIB governance)
- AC 39-7D (Airworthiness Directive compliance guidance)
