# Lesson 23: Density Altitude — Why Hot, High, and Humid Aircraft Underperform
## PPL_PA_I_F_01 | ACS Elements: PA.I.F.K1a, PA.I.F.K1b

### What This Lesson Covers
Why density altitude is a performance measurement — not a position measurement. The three compounding killers (hot, high, humid). The correct POH chart input sequence (pressure altitude, not field elevation). And the cold-weather altimetry trap that gets VFR pilots in mountainous terrain.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: Pressure Altitude vs. Density Altitude**
Pressure Altitude (PA): what the altimeter shows with 29.92 set.
PA = field elevation + [(29.92 - current altimeter setting) × 1,000 ft]

Density Altitude (DA): pressure altitude corrected for temperature.
DA = PA + (120 ft per °C ABOVE standard temperature at that altitude)
Standard temperature at sea level: 15°C. Lapse rate: 2°C per 1,000 ft.
Standard temp at 5,000 ft = 5°C. If actual OAT = 30°C, deviation = +25°C → DA penalty = 3,000 ft.
DA = 5,000 + 3,000 = 8,000 ft. The aircraft performs as if it is at 8,000 ft even though physically at 5,000 ft.

**RKP_02: Hot, High, and Humid — Three Independent Killers**
HIGH altitude → thinner air → less oxygen → less power/thrust/lift
HOT temperature → expanded air → fewer molecules per volume → engine, prop, wings all suffer
HUMID air → water vapor (mol weight 18) displaces heavier O2/N2 → air density decreases
Humidity adds ~2°C equivalent DA on very humid days — not captured by standard E6B.
Combined effect: compounding. 95°F at 4,500 ft on a humid day can produce DA of 9,000+ ft.

Performance impacts: longer takeoff roll, reduced climb rate, higher stall TAS, reduced service ceiling. Enter ALL performance charts with DA (or the correct PA + OAT inputs per POH).

**RKP_03: POH Chart Entry — The Right Inputs**
Step 1: Determine PRESSURE ALTITUDE (set 29.92, read altimeter — or calculate from field elevation + altimeter setting correction).
Step 2: Determine OAT in CELSIUS.
Step 3: Enter DA chart or POH performance chart with PA + OAT (many POH charts use these directly).
Step 4: Apply correction factors: headwind (−distance), tailwind (+distance), slope, surface.

CRITICAL INPUT ERROR: using field elevation instead of pressure altitude understates DA, producing takeoff distances that are shorter than reality. This causes runway overruns.

**RKP_04: Cold Weather Altimetry — From High to Low, Look Out Below**
Cold air = denser → BETTER performance → but altimeter reads HIGH (aircraft is lower than indicated).
Mnemonic: 'From HIGH to low (temp), look out BELOW' — in cold temps, the aircraft is below the altimeter reading.
In mountainous winter operations, correction can be 300-500 ft. AIM 7-2-3 covers IFR cold temperature corrections.
Hot air = less dense → performance degrades → altimeter reads LOW (aircraft is slightly higher than indicated but performance is still penalized).
