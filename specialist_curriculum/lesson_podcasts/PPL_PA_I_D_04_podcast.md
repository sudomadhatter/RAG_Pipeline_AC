# Lesson 18: Lost Procedures, GPS Navigation, and Diversion Decision-Making
## PPL_PA_I_D_04 | ACS Elements: PA.I.D.K6, PA.I.D.K7, PA.I.D.K8

### What This Lesson Covers
The human-factors-rich lessons that separate trained pilots from accident statistics: the 3C lost procedure, the four transponder emergency codes and what each triggers, the three-step in-flight diversion model with NTSB data context, and GPS/ADS-B operational awareness.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: The 3C Lost Procedure — Climb, Circle, Communicate, Conserve**
Lost? Stop and execute in order:
1. CLIMB — expand horizon for landmarks, improve VHF range, extend GPS reception. Stay above 91.119 obstacle clearance.
2. CIRCLE — orbit the last known landmark. Stop progressing the positional error.
3. COMMUNICATE — squawk 7700 if emergency. Call 121.5 MHz or nearest ATC. Announce type, altitude, last known position.
4. CONSERVE — manage fuel at best-range power. Do not press further into unknown terrain.

The DPE test: applicants who immediately grab the iPad are tested further: 'GPS is also offline.'

**RKP_02: Squawk Codes and What They Trigger**
- 1200 — Standard VFR (no ATC assignment)
- 7700 — EMERGENCY. Launches ATC emergency response, crash/fire equipment coordination.
- 7600 — RADIO FAILURE (NORDO). ATC will watch for 91.185 NORDO arrival procedures.
- 7500 — HIJACK. Military intercept coordinated immediately. Cannot be verbally canceled. NEVER test this code.

Memory: '75 = taken alive, 76 = sick (radio sick), 77 = heaven.'
ADS-B note: your squawk code is broadcast with your position to all traffic nearby.

**RKP_03: The Three-Step Diversion Decision Model**
1. SET YOUR TRIGGER BEFORE FLIGHT: ceiling <2,000 / visibility <5 miles at destination? Divert. Fuel at reserve? Divert. Set it now; enforce it later.
2. SELECT THE ALTERNATE: runway length sufficient? Fuel? VFR conditions? No SUA? Reachable with current fuel?
3. NAVIGATE TO THE ALTERNATE: direct-to on EFB, or pilotage (compass bearing to alternate, apply wind correction).

NTSB SS-15-01: VFR-into-IMC fatality rate is 11.2x higher than any other cause of fatal Part 91 accidents.
The fatal error: waiting until the trigger is overrun before deciding. Decide while options are still good.

**RKP_04: GPS, RAIM, WAAS, and ADS-B**
RAIM alert = insufficient satellite geometry for navigation integrity. Revert to pilotage or VOR immediately.
WAAS = FAA ground corrections improve GPS accuracy to ~3 meters. Required for LPV approaches. Optional for VFR.
ADS-B Out required per 14 CFR 91.225 in Class A, B, C, and E above 10,000 MSL.
ADS-B In = receive traffic + FIS-B weather (AC 00-63B governs weather display use).
Database currency: even VFR GPS needs current databases — outdated databases miss TFRs, show wrong airspace.

### Key Regulations
- 14 CFR 91.119 — Minimum safe altitudes (used in 3C Climb step)
- 14 CFR 91.183 — Transponder reporting requirements (7700 protocol)
- 14 CFR 91.185 — Two-way radio failure procedures (NORDO / 7600)
- 14 CFR 91.225 — ADS-B Out equipment requirements
- 14 CFR 91.227 — ADS-B performance requirements
- AC 90-100A — GPS receiver requirements and database currency
