# Lesson 25: Takeoff and Landing Performance — Charts, Factors, and Obstacle Clearance
## PPL_PA_I_F_03 | ACS Elements: PA.I.F.K3, PA.I.F.S2

### What This Lesson Covers
The three distinct POH performance numbers (ground roll, 50-foot obstacle, accelerate-stop), the four adjustment factors (wind, slope, surface, weight), landing energy physics (speed squared = distance squared), stabilized approach criteria with the 500-foot gate, and short/soft field technique differences.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: Three Takeoff Numbers — Know Which One Applies**
GROUND ROLL: wheels leave pavement. Used only to determine if the aircraft can physically lift off.
OVER 50-FOOT OBSTACLE: from brake release to 50 feet AGL passing over object. THIS is your clearance check.
ACCELERATE-STOP: from brake release to full stop after aborting at rotation speed. Used for rejected takeoff planning.

Landing parallel: LANDING ROLL vs. LANDING OVER 50-FOOT OBSTACLE. Same principle — obstacle number is always longer.
Most dangerous error: using ground roll to check obstacle clearance. Ground roll = liftoff. 50-ft obstacle = actual clearance.

**RKP_02: Four Performance Adjustment Factors**
WIND: headwind helps (reduces roll). Tailwind HURTS — a 10-knot tail doubles takeoff distance on some aircraft.
SLOPE: upslope increases takeoff roll. Downslope helps landing (natural braking).
SURFACE: wet grass = +15-25% over paved chart values.
WEIGHT: heavier = distance increases as weight ratio SQUARED. 10% heavier → ~21% more distance.
POH charts assume: dry, paved, hard surface, level, standard. Any deviation increases distance.

**RKP_03: Landing Energy and the 500-Foot Gate**
Kinetic energy = ½mv². Speed excess scales squared → 10% faster crossing the fence = 21% more landing roll.
Vref is the specific approach speed for the configuration — POH-specified. Exceed it and you carry extra energy onto the runway.
Stabilized approach: by 500 AGL (1,000 AGL IFR): within 10 kts of Vref, on glidepath, in landing config, power stabilized.
If NOT stabilized by 500 AGL → GO-AROUND. Always.
Float: aircraft flying level above runway because speed hasn't decayed. Never force it on. Carry power off, float it out, or go around.
Wet runway: braking reduced 60-70% over dry pavement. Wet landing = significantly longer roll.

**RKP_04: Short vs. Soft Field Technique**
Short Field Takeoff: max flaps per POH, full runway used, brakes held at full power, rotate at Vx, climb at Vx to obstacle, transition to Vy.
Short Field Landing: firm touchdown at numbers (no float), immediate max braking, flaps retracted after touchdown.
Soft Field Takeoff: nosewheel unloaded early, lift off as soon as possible, accelerate in ground effect to Vx/Vy.
Soft Field Landing: power-controlled gentle descent, minimum touchdown speed.
