## PA.I.B.S1: Locate and describe airplane airworthiness and registration information

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
During the practical test, the applicant must physically navigate the aircraft's cabin to locate the Airworthiness Certificate, demonstrating that it is visibly displayed at the cabin or cockpit entrance as required by 91.203(b). They must extract the Registration Certificate, verify the N-number matches the airframe, confirm the serial number matches the data plate, and prove it has not passed its 7-year expiration date. Finally, they must synthesize this to assure the evaluator that both documents are current, legal, and belong to the specific aircraft being tested.

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
#### A. The Practical Demonstration (91.203(b))
The evaluator is assessing not just rote regulatory memorization, but the applicant's physical familiarity with the aircraft and its compliance state. The applicant should immediately and efficiently locate the clear plastic sleeve—usually positioned near the pilot's left knee, on the kick panel, or on the aft bulkhead—containing the FAA Form 8100-2 (Airworthiness) and AC Form 8050-3 (Registration).

#### B. Verifying the Airworthiness Certificate
The applicant must demonstrate the ability to read and interpret the Airworthiness Certificate. They should physically point out:
* The specific N-number on the certificate.
* The manufacturer's serial number.
* The aircraft make and model.
* The Category (e.g., Normal/Utility).
* The date of issuance. Crucially, they must point out the lack of an expiration date, explicitly stating the legal caveat: "It remains valid indefinitely, as long as the aircraft is maintained in accordance with Parts 43 and 91, and remains registered in the United States".

#### C. Auditing the Registration Certificate
The applicant must then audit the Registration Certificate for validity. They must:
* Confirm the owner's name and address match the flight school or private owner.
* Locate the issue date and calculate the expiration date. The applicant must demonstrate knowledge of the 14 CFR 47.40 rule change, explaining to the examiner that newer registrations expire seven years after the last day of the month they were issued.
* Check the physical condition of the document; a standard photocopy is not legal for domestic flight (with the exception of the temporary pink slip for newly purchased aircraft).

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Assuming the Binder is Legal:** The applicant confidently points to the aircraft's bulky maintenance binder sitting on the backseat and says, "The airworthiness is safely stored in there." This is an instant failure. 91.203(b) explicitly requires it to be displayed legibly at the entrance.
* **Overlooking the Serial Number:** The applicant checks the N-number on the paperwork but fails to verify that the manufacturer's serial number printed on the forms actually matches the physical data plate riveted to the empennage of the aircraft.
