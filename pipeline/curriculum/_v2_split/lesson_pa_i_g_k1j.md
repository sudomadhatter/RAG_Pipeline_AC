## PA.I.G.K1j: Deicing and anti-icing

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
Deicing systems, such as pneumatic rubber boots, are reactive systems designed to physically break off and remove structural ice *after* it has accumulated. Anti-icing systems, such as electrical pitot heat or TKS weeping wings, are preventative systems designed to be activated *before* entering icing conditions to prohibit ice from adhering to the airframe.

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")

**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 91.527 explicitly prohibits takeoff in an aircraft that has frost, ice, or snow adhering to any propeller, windshield, or stabilizing or control surface.

**The "Why":** Structural ice accumulation fundamentally destroys the aerodynamic profile of the airfoil. It disrupts laminar airflow, causing early boundary layer separation. This drastically decreases the maximum coefficient of lift ($C_{L-MAX}$) while exponentially increasing both parasite and induced drag, leading to an unpredictably higher stall speed. Pneumatic boots operate by utilizing air from an engine-driven vacuum/pressure pump to rapidly inflate and deflate rubber bladders on the leading edge, physically cracking the brittle ice. Anti-ice fluid systems (like TKS weeping wings) pump a highly concentrated glycol-based chemical through microscopic laser-drilled holes in a titanium leading edge. This fluid chemically mixes with supercooled water droplets upon impact, lowering their freezing point so the moisture flows harmlessly off the trailing edge without crystallizing.

**Scenario Application:** A pilot is flying an aircraft equipped with a "non-hazard" (non-FIKI) weeping wing system and inadvertently flies into a cumulus cloud layer that produces rapid, clear ice accumulation. The pilot immediately activates the anti-ice system to its maximum flow rate. Because the system is not certified for prolonged flight in known icing, the pilot uses the temporary chemical protection to safely execute an immediate 180-degree turn to exit the cloud layer, preventing the ice accretion rate from overwhelming the fluid flow.

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **FIKI vs. Non-Hazard Certification:** Believing that having pneumatic boots or a weeping wing automatically makes the aircraft certified for Flight Into Known Icing (FIKI). Many general aviation systems are classified merely as "inadvertent encounter" systems. They lack the necessary fluid capacity, heated windshields, heated propellers, and redundant alternators required to legally or safely linger in icing conditions.
* **Misunderstanding Ground Holdover Times:** Assuming that spraying the aircraft with ground anti-icing fluid provides indefinite protection. Anti-ice fluids have strict, chemically dictated "holdover times" that degrade rapidly in heavy precipitation or high winds, leaving the aircraft completely vulnerable to structural icing if the departure is delayed.
