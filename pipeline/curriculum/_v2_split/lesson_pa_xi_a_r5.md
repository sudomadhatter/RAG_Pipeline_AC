### PA.XI.A.R5: Night currency versus proficiency.

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
Legal currency is a minimum binary standard set by the FAA: a pilot must have performed 3 takeoffs and landings to a full stop within the preceding 90 days (from 1 hour after sunset to 1 hour before sunrise) to legally carry passengers at night. Proficiency, however, is a pilot's actual competence and safety level in current conditions. A pilot may be legally current because they executed three landings on a calm, clear night 89 days ago, but they may completely lack the proficiency required to safely execute a night landing in gusty crosswinds or deteriorating weather today.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 1.1, 14 CFR 61.57(b), 14 CFR 91.209.

**The Science & Legal Nuance:** The FAA utilizes three distinct legal definitions of "night," which frequently confuse pilots and are heavily scrutinized during checkrides:

| Regulatory Definition | Timeframe | Legal Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **14 CFR 1.1 (General Definition)** | Evening Civil Twilight to Morning Civil Twilight (Sun is 6 degrees below horizon). | Logging "Night Flight Time" in a pilot logbook. |
| **14 CFR 91.209 (Aircraft Lights)** | Sunset to Sunrise. | Determines when physical aircraft position lights must be illuminated. |
| **14 CFR 61.57(b) (Passenger Currency)** | 1 Hour After Sunset to 1 Hour Before Sunrise. | Determines when 3 full-stop landings are required to carry passengers. |

The FAA requires currency landings to occur in "deep night" (1 hour after sunset) to ensure the pilot's depth perception is truly tested without the aid of twilight horizon lines. Furthermore, the legal interpretations in *Kortokrax (2006)* and *Schaffner (2014)* clarify the definition of a "passenger." The FAA ruled that an authorized Flight Instructor (CFI) providing flight training is *not* a passenger. Therefore, a CFI who has lost their 61.57(b) night currency can legally take a student up at night to teach them, and the student's landings do not violate the passenger-carrying rule, because neither individual is legally classified as a passenger during the training event.

**The "Why":** Risk management dictates that minimum legal compliance does not guarantee survival. Night flying skills, particularly instrument scanning and depth perception in the landing flare, atrophy rapidly. A pilot must establish personal minimums that far exceed the 90-day currency rule to ensure absolute proficiency.

**Scenario Application:** A private pilot wants to take their spouse on a night flight to a local restaurant. The pilot checks their logbook and sees they completed three full-stop night landings exactly 85 days ago in perfect VFR conditions. They are legally *current* under 61.57(b). However, tonight it is overcast, raining lightly, and there is a 12-knot crosswind. Applying sound risk management, the pilot recognizes they are highly *unproficient* for these specific conditions. They decide to cancel the passenger flight, opting instead to fly solo or hire a CFI to regain proficiency in adverse night conditions before taking passengers.

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The Twilight Currency Trap:** Performing three touch-and-go landings 15 minutes after sunset, logging them as night currency, and then illegally carrying passengers in deep night a month later. 61.57(b) explicitly requires the landings to occur 1 hour after sunset, and they must be to a full stop.
* **Logging Confusion:** Believing you cannot log "night flight time" in your logbook until 1 hour after sunset. Night time logging actually begins much earlier, at the end of evening civil twilight (usually 20-30 minutes after sunset, depending on latitude).
* **The Touch-and-Go Error:** Performing night touch-and-goes to regain passenger currency. Day currency allows touch-and-goes (in tricycle gear aircraft); night currency *always* requires a full stop to ensure the pilot can successfully manage the entire deceleration phase without visual cues.
