## PA.I.A.S1: Apply requirements to act as pilot-in-command (PIC) under Visual Flight Rules (VFR).
> **Context:** Preflight Preparation > Pilot Qualifications > PIC under VFR

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
The applicant must demonstrate the ability to synthesize regulations, legal interpretations, and risk management principles to make a definitive Go/No-Go decision. When presented with a flight scenario, the pilot must evaluate their own currency (61.56/57), the aircraft's status (documents/inspections), and the legality of the operation (61.113 - compensation/hire) to determine if the flight can be conducted safely and legally.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**A. The "Can I Do This?" Algorithm**
To determine flight legality, the pilot must traverse a logical decision tree:
* **Pilot Legality:**
  * Do I have my certificate, medical/BasicMed, and photo ID?
  * Am I current for the conditions (Day/Night, Passenger/Solo)?
  * Have I had a flight review?
* **Aircraft Legality:**
  * Is the aircraft airworthy? (ARROW documents).
  * Does it have the required radio licenses (if international)?
* **Operational Legality (The Privileges Test):**
  * Is there compensation involved? (Reimbursement is compensation).
  * If yes, is there an exception? (Pro Rata Share, Business Incidental, etc.).
  * If Pro Rata: Is there a Common Purpose?
* **Safety (The Proficiency Test):**
  * Do I meet my personal minimums?

**B. Scenario Analysis: The "Canoe Club" (Based on Chero Interpretation)**
* **Scenario:** A local canoe club asks a private pilot to fly three members to a canoe race 200 miles away. The pilot is not a member of the club and has no interest in canoeing. The club members offer to pay for the fuel and oil for the trip. The pilot agrees.
* **Analysis:**
  * **Compensation Check:** The pilot is receiving reimbursement for fuel and oil; this constitutes compensation.
  * **Exception Check:** 61.113(c) allows pro rata reimbursement.
  * **Common Purpose Test:** Does the pilot have a reason to go to the destination other than transporting the passengers? No. The pilot is going solely because the club asked.
* **Verdict: ILLEGAL.** The flight lacks a bona fide common purpose. The pilot is essentially acting as a commercial charter operator (Part 135) without a certificate. To make this legal, the pilot would have to pay 100% of the costs.

**C. Scenario Analysis: The Business Trip**
* **Scenario:** A real estate agent (Private Pilot) flies their own Cessna 182 to a remote property to show it to a potential buyer. The agent invites the buyer to fly along in the Cessna. The agent's real estate firm reimburses the agent for the cost of the flight.
* **Analysis:**
  * **Compensation Check:** The agent is receiving reimbursement from the firm.
  * **Exception Check:** 61.113(b) allows compensation if the flight is "incidental to business."
  * **Condition Check:** The flight is incidental (transport to the sale).
  * **The Passenger Trap:** 61.113(b)(2) states the aircraft does not carry passengers or property for compensation or hire. Since the agent is being reimbursed (compensated) for the flight, carrying the client (passenger) violates this clause.
* **Verdict: ILLEGAL.** The agent can fly solo and be reimbursed. Or, the agent can take the client but cannot accept reimbursement (must pay 100% of costs personally).

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The "I'm not making a profit" Defense:** Pilots often believe that if they don't profit, it's legal. The FAA regulates compensation, not profit; reimbursement is compensation.
* **Ignoring the "Common Purpose" Doctrine:** This is the most frequent point of failure in oral exams. The pilot must articulate why they are making the flight independent of the passengers.
