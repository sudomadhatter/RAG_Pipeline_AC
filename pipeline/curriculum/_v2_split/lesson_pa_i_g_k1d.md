## PA.I.G.K1d: Landing gear

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
Landing gear systems support the aircraft during ground operations, provide steering capability, and absorb the severe kinetic energy generated during touchdown. They are classified as either fixed or retractable, and typically employ spring-steel legs, bungee cords, or oleo-pneumatic shock struts to safely dissipate impact forces into the airframe.

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")

**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 91.205(b) strictly requires that any aircraft equipped with a retractable landing gear system must have an operable landing gear position indicator for VFR day flight operations to prevent gear-up landings.

**The "Why":** The physics of a landing touchdown involve massive deceleration forces ($F=ma$). Spring-steel gear absorbs this energy by flexing outward, transferring the shock across a wider time interval. High-performance aircraft utilize oleo-pneumatic struts. An oleo strut contains two telescoping cylinders filled with compressed nitrogen gas and MIL-H-5606 hydraulic fluid. During ground taxi, the compressed nitrogen acts as a pneumatic spring to cushion minor bumps. Upon the severe impact of landing, the lower cylinder is forced upward, driving the incompressible hydraulic fluid through a tiny calibrated orifice (regulated by a tapered metering pin) into the upper chamber. This process forces high-speed fluid friction, cleanly converting the aircraft's vertical kinetic energy into thermal energy and preventing the aircraft from bouncing back into the air. Retractable systems employ hydraulic actuators or electric motors. To prevent ground retraction, they are equipped with squat switches (microswitches on the torque links) that open the electrical circuit when the strut is compressed by the aircraft's weight.

**Scenario Application:** A pilot is flying a multi-engine aircraft when the hydraulic pump shears its drive spline, rendering the hydraulic system inoperative. The landing gear must be manually extended using an emergency free-fall or hand-pump system. Because aerodynamic drag forces the gear backward, the pilot must ensure the gear extends fully until the mechanical over-center locks engage. The pilot verifies the three green position indicators before touchdown, knowing that an unlocked gear will instantly fold backward upon the application of weight and drag during the landing roll.

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Flat Oleo Struts:** Failing to evaluate the chrome piston extension during the preflight inspection. If the strut is completely compressed (flat), the nitrogen charge has leaked. Landing on a flat strut provides zero shock absorption, transferring the entire kinetic load directly into the wing spar, causing severe structural damage.
* **Squat Switch Complacency:** Assuming the squat switch is an infallible safeguard. If the switch is bypassed, misrigged, or frozen closed with ice, placing the gear selector switch to the "UP" position while taxiing will instantly collapse the aircraft onto the runway.
