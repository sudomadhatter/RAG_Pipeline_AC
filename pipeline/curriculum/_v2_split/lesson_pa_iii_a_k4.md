### PA.III.A.K4: Appropriate use of transponders

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
A transponder allows ATC secondary surveillance radar to positively identify and track an aircraft's altitude and position. A private pilot must operate the transponder in Mode C or Mode S (Altitude Reporting) at all times in controlled airspace unless otherwise requested by ATC. The pilot must memorize standard squawk codes: 1200 (VFR), 7500 (Hijack), 7600 (Lost Communications), and 7700 (Emergency).

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 91.215 (ATC transponder and altitude reporting equipment and use) and 14 CFR 91.225 (ADS-B Out equipment and use).

**The "Why":** Primary radar only bounces radio waves off the metal skin of an aircraft, providing a raw, 2D return with no altitude data and severe limitations regarding weather attenuation. The transponder resolves this via an active interrogation-reply system. When the ATC secondary radar interrogates the aircraft on 1030 MHz, the transponder replies on 1090 MHz, transmitting a digital data packet containing the 4-digit octal code and the pressure altitude provided by the blind encoder. 14 CFR 91.215 mandates that if the aircraft is equipped with an operable transponder, it must be turned ON and replying with altitude telemetry at all times. This data feeds into Traffic Collision Avoidance Systems (TCAS) for collision avoidance logic.

**Scenario Application:** A pilot is departing a non-towered airport located beneath the outer ring of a Class B airspace shelf. Before taking the active runway, the pilot ensures the transponder is set to ALT (or ON for modern digital units) and squawking 1200. This ensures that the TRACON controllers, and the TCAS systems of the airliners descending overhead, can see the exact altitude relative to the shelf, preventing an alert or a mid-air collision.

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The "Standby" Habit:** Taxiing with the transponder on "Standby" and forgetting to switch to "Altitude" before takeoff. Modern ASDE-X surface radar relies on transponder returns to track ground movements; therefore, the AIM dictates the transponder should be ON at all times during taxi.
* **Code Rolling Errors:** Accidentally passing through 7500, 7600, or 7700 while turning the mechanical dials of older transponders. This triggers a momentary alarm in the ATC facility, leading to unnecessary radio inquiries or intercept protocols.
