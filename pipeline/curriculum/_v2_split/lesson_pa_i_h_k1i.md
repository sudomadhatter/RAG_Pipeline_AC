## PA.I.H.K1i: Dehydration and nutrition

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
Dehydration is a critical depletion of bodily fluids that causes headaches, severe fatigue, cramps, and dizziness, while poor nutrition leads to hypoglycemia (low blood sugar), resulting in weakness, tremors, and fainting. Pilots should proactively drink 2 to 4 quarts of water daily and consume balanced meals prior to flight to maintain the high cognitive function required for safe aircraft operation.

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** Concept based; relates to 14 CFR 61.53 fitness for flight.

**The "Why":** The atmosphere at altitude is exceptionally dry, often containing less than 10% relative humidity. This causes rapid "insensible fluid loss"—water lost continuously through respiration and skin evaporation without the pilot consciously noticing. A loss of just 2% of body weight in water leads to a measurable 10-20% degradation in cognitive performance and psychomotor tracking skills. Furthermore, the brain requires constant glucose (from nutrition) and hydration to fuel neural synapses. Hypoglycemia from skipping meals causes anxiety and a narrowing of the visual field. When combined with dehydration, the blood volume decreases and thickens, making it substantially harder for the heart to pump oxygen-rich blood to the brain, which artificially lowers the pilot's tolerance to both hypoxia and G-forces.

**Scenario Application:** A pilot spends three hours meticulously pre-flighting their aircraft on a hot 95°F ramp without drinking any water, then immediately launches for a VFR cross-country flight at 8,500 feet MSL. The extremely dry air aloft compounds the existing dehydration. The pilot develops a severe headache, becomes highly lethargic, and struggles to accurately calculate top-of-descent profiles. This profound impairment could easily escalate to spatial disorientation or a Loss of Control - Inflight (LOC-I) incident.

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The "Thirst" Indicator:** Waiting until you feel thirsty to drink water. By the time the thirst mechanism engages, the body is already significantly dehydrated.
* **Diuretic Consumption:** Relying on coffee, tea, or sodas before a flight. Caffeine acts as a mild diuretic, accelerating fluid loss in an already desiccating, high-altitude environment.
