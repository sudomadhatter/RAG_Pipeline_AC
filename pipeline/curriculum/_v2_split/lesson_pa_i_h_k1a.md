## PA.I.H.K1a: Hypoxia

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
Hypoxia is a physiological state of oxygen deficiency in the body sufficient to impair function of the brain and other vital organs. There are four main types: hypoxic (insufficient oxygen available), hypemic (blood cannot carry oxygen), stagnant (blood is not moving), and histotoxic (cells are unable to use oxygen). Symptoms include cyanosis, visual impairment, euphoria, and impaired judgment, requiring the immediate donning of supplemental oxygen and an emergency descent.

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 91.211 (Supplemental oxygen).

**The "Why":** Hypoxic hypoxia is driven by Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures. While the atmosphere remains approximately 21% oxygen regardless of altitude, the overall barometric pressure decreases as altitude increases. This reduces the partial pressure of oxygen, meaning there is insufficient pressure to force oxygen across the alveolar membrane in the lungs into the bloodstream.  The brain, which consumes nearly 20% of the body's oxygen, is highly sensitive to this deprivation.

The Time of Useful Consciousness (TUC) drops exponentially at higher altitudes, as shown in the data below:

| Altitude (MSL) | Time of Useful Consciousness (TUC) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 20,000 feet | 30 minutes or more |
| 22,000 feet | 5 to 10 minutes |
| 25,000 feet | 3 to 5 minutes |
| 28,000 feet | 2.5 to 3 minutes |
| 30,000 feet | 1 to 2 minutes |

**Scenario Application:** A pilot is flying an unpressurized aircraft at 14,500 feet MSL. After 45 minutes, they begin to experience a false sense of well-being (euphoria) and a dull headache. Although legally they are only required to use oxygen after 30 minutes above 12,500 feet, their physiological tolerance is lower today. They fail to recognize the cognitive degradation and miss several radio calls before a passenger points out the blue tint of their fingernails (cyanosis). The pilot must immediately apply 100% supplemental oxygen, check the regulator and flow indicator, and initiate a descent below 10,000 feet.

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The "Legal is Safe" Fallacy:** Assuming that because 14 CFR 91.211 does not mandate oxygen below 12,500 feet MSL for less than 30 minutes, hypoxia cannot occur. Night vision degrades as low as 5,000 feet MSL due to the high oxygen demand of the retina's rod cells.
* **Symptom Misattribution:** Mistaking the euphoria of hypoxia for genuine comfort, leading the pilot to ignore objective signs of physiological failure until unconsciousness is imminent.
