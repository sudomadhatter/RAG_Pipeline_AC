## PA.I.H.K1e: Motion sickness

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
Motion sickness is a debilitating condition caused by the brain receiving conflicting messages regarding spatial orientation, typically a severe mismatch between what the eyes see and what the inner ear feels. Symptoms include excessive yawning, pallor, cold sweating, nausea, and vomiting. Corrective actions include opening fresh air vents, instructing the affected person to focus on a distant, fixed point outside the aircraft, minimizing head movements, and keeping them occupied with simple tasks.

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 61.53 (Prohibition on operations during medical deficiency).

**The "Why":** Motion sickness (airsickness) is the direct result of sensory conflict. The central nervous system struggles to integrate vastly different data streams from the visual system and the vestibular system. Evolutionary biology suggests that the brain interprets this profound sensory discrepancy as a neurotoxin hallucination, triggering a physiological purging response (vomiting). The pilot in command is inherently less susceptible than passengers because the active control of the aircraft allows the pilot's brain to anticipate the motion, perfectly aligning the visual expectations with the vestibular reality.

**Scenario Application:** During a turbulent, hot summer afternoon flight, a passenger in the right seat begins breathing rapidly, sweating, and staring down at an iPad. The pilot recognizes the onset of motion sickness. The pilot immediately directs the passenger to put the screen away and look out the window at the distant, stable horizon (re-aligning the visual data with the vestibular data), opens the eyeball air vents to provide a cooling sensory distraction, and initiates a climb to find smoother air.

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Masking the Problem:** Ignoring early prodromal signs (yawning, swallowing, loss of color) until the passenger is actively vomiting. Early intervention with outside visual reference is critical.
* **Dangerous Self-Medication:** Pilots attempting to use over-the-counter anti-emetics (like Dramamine, meclizine, or scopolamine patches) to cure their own motion sickness. These drugs have highly sedating side effects that impair cognitive function and reaction times. Their use places the pilot in direct violation of 14 CFR 61.53 and the FAA's "Do Not Fly" medication guidelines.
