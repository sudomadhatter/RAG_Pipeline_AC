## PA.I.A.K5: Part 68 BasicMed privileges and limitations.
> **Context:** Preflight Preparation > Pilot Qualifications > BasicMed

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
BasicMed is an alternative to Part 67 medical certification, established by the FAA Extension, Safety, and Security Act of 2016. To qualify, a pilot must hold a U.S. driver's license and have held a valid FAA medical certificate after July 14, 2006. Requirements include completing a Comprehensive Medical Examination Checklist (CMEC) with a state-licensed physician every 48 months and an online medical education course every 24 calendar months. Operations are restricted to aircraft with a maximum certificated takeoff weight of 6,000 lbs and 6 occupants, flying no faster than 250 knots and no higher than 18,000 feet MSL.

**A. The Legal Basis (Part 68 & PL 114-190)**
BasicMed is a statutory relief program; unlike Part 67, which is purely regulatory, BasicMed is codified in Public Law 114-190 and implemented via 14 CFR Part 68, providing a robust alternative for General Aviation pilots to fly without the bureaucracy of the Aviation Medical Examiner (AME) system.

**B. Qualification Requirements**
To operate under BasicMed, the pilot must meet four specific criteria (61.113(i) & 68.3):
* **Prior Medical:** The pilot must have held a valid FAA medical certificate (First, Second, or Third Class) at any point after July 14, 2006. This medical must not have been suspended, revoked, or withdrawn.
* **Driver's License:** The pilot must hold a current and valid U.S. driver's license. The license acts as a proxy for basic functional capability. Any restriction on the driver's license (e.g., corrective lenses) applies to the pilot privileges.
* **The Physical (CMEC):** The pilot must undergo a physical examination by a state-licensed physician (MD or DO). The physician must complete the FAA Comprehensive Medical Examination Checklist (CMEC). This exam is valid for 48 months to the day.
* **The Education Course:** The pilot must complete an FAA-approved online medical education course (available via AOPA or Mayo Clinic). This course completion is valid for 24 calendar months.

**Table: BasicMed vs. Third Class Medical**

| Feature | BasicMed | Third Class Medical |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Exam Provider | State-Licensed Physician (MD/DO) | FAA Aviation Medical Examiner (AME) |
| Exam Frequency | 48 months (to the day) | 60 months (under 40) / 24 months (40+) |
| Education Req. | Online Course every 24 cal. months | None |
| Aircraft Weight | Max 6,000 lbs MTOW | No Limit (Type Rating dependent) |
| Occupants | Max 6 (5 passengers + pilot) | No Limit (Type Rating dependent) |
| Altitude | Max 18,000 ft MSL | No Limit |
| Speed | Max 250 knots IAS | No Limit (Mach 1.0+) |


**C. Operating Limitations (61.113(i))**
When flying under BasicMed, the pilot is subject to strict operational envelopes:
* **Aircraft Eligibility:** The aircraft authorized to carry not more than 6 occupants and with a maximum certificated takeoff weight of not more than 6,000 pounds. 
* **The "Seat Removal" Fallacy:** A pilot cannot fly a Piper Navajo (8 seats, >6000 lbs) simply by removing seats to make it a 6-seater. The limitation applies to the aircraft's type certification specifications.
* **Flight Parameters:** Flight is restricted to 250 knots indicated airspeed or less and 18,000 feet MSL or below. Operations must be within the United States (unless specifically authorized by a foreign country, e.g., the Bahamas).
* **No Compensation or Hire:** BasicMed cannot be used for commercial operations. However, Flight Instructors may instruct under BasicMed because they are being paid for instruction, not for piloting the aircraft (acting as PIC).

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The Safety Pilot Loophole:** A Safety Pilot is a required crewmember under 91.109. Therefore, a Safety Pilot may operate under BasicMed because they are a required crewmember, not a passenger.
* **Special Issuance Conditions:** Certain conditions (mental health disorders, neurological disorders, cardiac conditions) require a one-time Special Issuance from the FAA before a pilot can transition to BasicMed. A pilot cannot simply bypass the FAA if they have a new diagnosis of epilepsy or a heart valve replacement; they must get the "golden ticket" (one-time SI) first.
