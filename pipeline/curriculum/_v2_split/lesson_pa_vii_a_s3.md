### PA.VII.A.S3: Establish and maintain slow flight airspeed.

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
To successfully establish slow flight, the pilot must clear the airspace and gradually reduce power while simultaneously increasing back pressure to maintain altitude as the aircraft decelerates. As the airspeed approaches the target (just above the stall warning), the pilot must actively add power to arrest the deceleration and use rudder to maintain coordinated flight. The Airman Certification Standards require maintaining altitude within 100 feet, heading within 10 degrees, and airspeed at +10/-0 knots of the assigned target.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** Evaluated under the FAA-S-ACS-6C Practical Test Standards.

**The "Why":** Demonstrates the pilot's mastery of energy management, divided attention, and understanding of the reversed command physics envelope.

**Scenario Application:** Executing a stabilized, slow-speed holding pattern while awaiting clearance to land at a busy non-towered airport.

**The Kinematics of the Transition**
The execution of the slow flight maneuver is a delicate exercise in dynamic energy management. The pilot is tasked with bleeding off kinetic energy (airspeed) while maintaining potential energy (altitude). As the throttle is reduced, the thrust vector shrinks. To prevent a descent, the pilot must increase the coefficient of lift ($C_L$) by increasing the AOA, which is accomplished by smoothly applying back pressure on the elevator.

As the aircraft slows and the AOA increases, the pilot must anticipate the massive increase in induced drag. If the pilot waits until the aircraft reaches the target airspeed to add power, the deceleration inertia and the induced drag will cause the aircraft to sink rapidly below the assigned altitude. A proficient pilot anticipates this by smoothly advancing the throttle just before reaching the target airspeed, stabilizing the aircraft on the back side of the power curve.

During this transition, control effectiveness degrades significantly. The dynamic pressure of the relative wind flowing over the ailerons, elevator, and rudder is reduced due to the low airspeed. Consequently, the pilot must use larger, yet smoother, control deflections to achieve the desired aircraft response. Abrupt control inputs at these speeds can easily un-stall the boundary layer or induce adverse yaw.

**Environmental Factors and ACS Tolerances**
The Airman Certification Standards (FAA-S-ACS-6C) dictate strict tolerances for this maneuver. A critical note within the ACS states that evaluators must recognize environmental factors, such as atmospheric turbulence or thermal activity. A sudden vertical gust can momentarily change the relative wind vector, increasing the AOA just enough to trigger a momentary activation of the stall warning horn. The ACS clarifies that if the applicant recognizes this momentary indication and promptly makes an appropriate correction (such as a slight relaxation of back pressure), it does not constitute unsatisfactory performance. This highlights the FAA's focus on aeronautical decision-making and risk management over robotic perfection.

| ACS Standard Parameter | Private Pilot Tolerance | Commercial Pilot Tolerance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Altitude** | $\pm$ 100 feet | $\pm$ 50 feet |
| **Airspeed** | +10 / -0 knots | +5 / -0 knots |
| **Heading** | $\pm$ 10 degrees | $\pm$ 10 degrees |
| **Bank Angle** | $\pm$ 10 degrees | $\pm$ 5 degrees |

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Chasing the Airspeed Indicator:** Fixating on the airspeed gauge rather than flying by outside visual references and the physical "feel" of the aircraft, resulting in erratic pitch oscillations (pilot-induced oscillations).
* **Configuration Sequencing:** Failing to anticipate the ballooning effect when extending flaps. As flaps are lowered, the camber of the wing increases, which instantly increases lift and drag. If the pilot does not anticipate this with forward yoke pressure, the aircraft will climb out of the ACS altitude tolerance.
