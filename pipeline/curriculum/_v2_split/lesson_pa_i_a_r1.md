## PA.I.A.R1: Proficiency versus currency.
> **Context:** Preflight Preparation > Pilot Qualifications > Proficiency vs Currency

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
Currency is the legal compliance with regulations (meeting the minimum requirements of 61.56 and 61.57). Proficiency is the pilot's actual ability to conduct the flight safely and competently. A pilot may be legally current having performed three landings 89 days ago in calm winds, but they may lack the proficiency to safely handle a crosswind landing today. Proficiency degrades over time and requires regular practice beyond the regulatory minimums.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**A. The Data (NTSB Analysis)**
The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) consistently cites "Loss of Control In-Flight" (LOC-I) as the leading cause of fatal General Aviation accidents. A recurring theme in these accident reports is the "Current but Not Proficient" pilot.
* **Case Study (WPR13FA289):** A pilot operating a multi-engine aircraft experienced an engine failure. The pilot was legally current, having met the minimum takeoff and landing requirements. However, the pilot had flown very few hours in the preceding years. The NTSB determined that "lack of proficiency" was a contributing factor to the loss of control. The pilot simply did not have the muscle memory or cognitive processing speed to handle the emergency, despite being "legal".

**B. The PAVE Checklist Application**
Risk management requires a brutally honest self-assessment using the PAVE model:
* **P (Pilot):** The "IMSAFE" checklist covers physical readiness, but proficiency falls under the pilot's competence. The pilot must ask: "When was the last time I flew in these conditions?"
* **The "Rust" Factor:** Aeronautical skills do not degrade linearly. Cognitive Skills (IFR procedures, emergency flows) degrade rapidly, often within weeks. Motor Skills (landings, steep turns) are more durable but lose precision.
* **Experience Trap:** High-time pilots are often the most vulnerable to the proficiency trap, assuming their 5,000 hours of past experience compensates for 6 months of inactivity.

**C. Personal Minimums**
To mitigate the gap between legal currency and safety, pilots must establish Personal Minimums. These are self-imposed rules that exceed FAA regulations.
* **Regulatory Minimum:** 3 landings in 90 days.
* **Personal Minimum:** "If I haven't flown in 30 days, I will fly with an instructor."
* **Regulatory Minimum:** VFR visibility 3 miles.
* **Personal Minimum:** "I will not fly VFR cross-country with less than 5 miles visibility."
* Developing these minimums before the flight prevents "mission creep" and rationalization when under pressure.

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The "Just One Flight" Fallacy:** "I haven't flown in 88 days, so I'll just take my family up for a quick spin before my currency expires." This is a high-risk behavior; the pressure to complete the flight to "save" the currency often leads to poor decision-making.
* **Weather Dependency:** Being current in calm winds does not imply proficiency in gusty crosswinds. Currency is a binary state (yes/no); proficiency is a sliding scale dependent on conditions.
