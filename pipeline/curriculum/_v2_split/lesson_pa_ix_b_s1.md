### PA.IX.B.S1: Establish and maintain the recommended best glide airspeed, ±10 knots.

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
Upon recognizing an emergency, the applicant must physically demonstrate the skill to immediately pitch the aircraft's nose to capture the manufacturer's recommended best glide airspeed (Vg). The applicant must then utilize the elevator trim tab to relieve all control pressures, ensuring the aircraft maintains Vg within a strict tolerance of ±10 knots while they divide their attention outside the aircraft to select a field and inside to run checklists.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")

##### A. The Mechanics of Airspeed Control
In standard aviation aerodynamics, pitch controls airspeed, and power controls altitude. When the power variable is entirely eliminated during an engine failure, pitch becomes the sole determinant of airspeed. The pilot must intuitively know the exact visual pitch picture relative to the natural horizon that yields best glide speed. Relying solely on the airspeed indicator inside the cockpit is a hazardous skill error; pressure-based instruments possess inherent latency. The pilot must physically push or pull the yoke to establish the visual pitch picture immediately, allow the aircraft's inertia to stabilize, and then cross-reference the airspeed indicator to make necessary micro-adjustments.

##### B. The Absolute Necessity of Trim
Maintaining precise airspeed during a high-workload emergency is physically and mentally impossible if the pilot is continually wrestling with the flight controls. The skill of trimming is a critical workload management tool. By adjusting the elevator trim tab, the pilot alters the aerodynamic neutral point of the elevator control surface. Once perfectly trimmed for Vg, the aircraft inherently wants to fly at that specific angle of attack. If a gust of wind displaces the nose, natural aerodynamic damping will smoothly return the aircraft to the trimmed airspeed. This allows the pilot to momentarily remove their hands from the yoke to switch fuel tanks, activate the primer, tune the radio to 121.5, or pull out the emergency checklist without the aircraft entering a dangerous phugoid oscillation or an inadvertent aerodynamic stall.

##### C. Testing Standards and Tolerances
The Airman Certification Standards (ACS) mandate a strict flight tolerance of ±10 knots for this specific maneuver. Allowing the airspeed to decay 10 knots below Vg pushes the aircraft precariously close to the region of reversed command and the critical angle of attack, risking a stall while maneuvering. Allowing the airspeed to increase 10 knots above Vg exponentially increases parasite drag, robbing the aircraft of vital glide distance and potential energy. The evaluator actively assesses the applicant's ability to lock in this airspeed early in the maneuver; constant chasing of the needle demonstrates a fundamental lack of aerodynamic control and trim utilization.

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The Death Grip**: A pilot firmly grips the yoke, fighting the aircraft's natural aerodynamic tendencies. Because they do not use trim, the absolute moment they look down at the checklist to troubleshoot, they inadvertently pull back on the yoke, bleeding off 15 knots of airspeed.
* **Fixation on the Panel**: Staring strictly at the airspeed indicator while trying to dial in the exact speed, completely losing situational awareness of the outside horizon, terrain elevation, and aircraft bank angle.
