## PA.I.A.K4: Documents required to exercise private pilot privileges.
> **Context:** Preflight Preparation > Pilot Qualifications > Required Documents

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
To act as pilot in command, a private pilot must have three specific documents in their physical possession or readily accessible in the aircraft: (1) A valid Pilot Certificate, (2) A valid U.S. government-issued photo identification (such as a driver's license or passport), and (3) A valid Medical Certificate or BasicMed documentation. If the flight leaves the United States (e.g., to Canada or the Bahamas), the pilot additionally requires a Restricted Radiotelephone Operator Permit, and the aircraft requires a Radio Station License.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
> Image of pilot holding FAA certificate and US passport on ramp

**A. The "Big Three" (61.3)**
14 CFR 61.3 serves as the primary enforcement tool for ramp checks. It mandates that no person may serve as a required pilot flight crewmember unless they have the following documents "in that person's physical possession or readily accessible in the aircraft".
* **Pilot Certificate:** This must be the plastic card issued by the FAA. Paper certificates are no longer valid for exercise of privileges.
* **Photo Identification:** 14 CFR 61.3(a)(2) lists the acceptable forms of identification:
  * A valid driver's license (or permit) issued by a State, the District of Columbia, or a territory of the U.S.
  * A valid U.S. Armed Forces identification card.
  * A valid passport (U.S. or foreign).
  * A credential with a photograph issued by the Federal government/TSA (e.g., TWIC card).
* **Medical Qualification:** This can be a Part 67 Medical Certificate or, if operating under BasicMed, the pilot must carry the course completion certificate. The CMEC (checklist) is kept in the logbook, but the course certificate must be accessible.

**B. International Requirements (FCC & 47 CFR)**
While FAA regulations do not require a radio license for domestic VFR flight, the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) regulations apply to international operations.
* **The Aircraft (Station License):** 47 CFR 87.18(b) states that aircraft operating domestically are "licensed by rule" and do not need a paper license. However, if the aircraft makes international flights or communications, it must hold an individual Aircraft Radio Station License. This license is valid for 10 years.
* **The Pilot (Restricted Permit):** 47 CFR 13.9 and § 87.87 require that at least one person on board an aircraft flying internationally hold a Restricted Radiotelephone Operator Permit (RR). This permit is valid for the lifetime of the holder and requires no test—only an application (FCC Form 605) and fee.
* **Nuance:** A flight from Florida to the Bahamas requires both the Aircraft Station License (for the plane) and the Restricted Permit (for the pilot). A flight from Florida to Georgia requires neither.

**C. Logbook Requirements (61.51)**
There is a persistent myth that a private pilot must carry their logbook at all times.
* **The Rule:** A private pilot is not required to carry a logbook on board the aircraft.
* **Exceptions:**
  * **Student Pilots:** Must carry their logbook on solo cross-country flights to show endorsements (61.51(i)(2)).
  * **Recreational Pilots:** Must carry their logbook with endorsements if flying beyond 50 NM from the departure airport.
* **Proof of Currency:** While the logbook need not be in the plane, the pilot must be able to present it upon request to the FAA or NTSB within a reasonable time to prove compliance with recency of experience requirements (61.51).

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **"Readily Accessible" Definition:** The FAA interprets "readily accessible" to mean physically retrievable during the flight. A license in a flight bag in the back seat is accessible. A license left in the pilot's car or at home is a violation of 61.3.
* **Expired Driver's License:** 14 CFR 61.3(a)(2) explicitly requires the photo ID to be valid. If a pilot's driver's license expires, they cannot legally fly, even if their pilot certificate and medical are current. The ID serves as the validation of the pilot's identity.
