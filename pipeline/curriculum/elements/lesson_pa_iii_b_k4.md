### PA.III.B.K4: Use of automated weather and airport information

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
Pilots obtain real-time terminal weather and airport information via automated broadcasts like ATIS, AWOS, and ASOS. The pilot tunes the published VHF frequency to receive critical data including wind, visibility, cloud layers, temperature, dew point, altimeter setting, and active runways. This information dictates pattern entry, runway selection, and performance calculations prior to entering the terminal area.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** AIM 7-1-12 (Weather Observing Programs).

**The "Why":** Automated Surface Observing Systems (ASOS) and Automated Weather Observing Systems (AWOS) utilize a suite of sensors (anemometers, ceilometers, forward-scatter visibility sensors) to provide objective atmospheric data. This data is critical for the physics of landing. The altimeter setting calibrates the barometric altimeter to indicate true elevation above mean sea level, ensuring terrain clearance. Temperature and dewpoint data define the air density (density altitude) and the probability of carburetor icing. Automatic Terminal Information Service (ATIS) adds a human-recorded layer at towered airports, synthesizing the automated weather with Notices to Air Missions (NOTAMs), runway closures, and approach types in use, assigning a phonetic letter (e.g., "Information Alpha") to guarantee the pilot and controller share the exact same operational dataset.

**Scenario Application:** Approaching a coastal airport, a pilot tunes the ASOS frequency 20 miles out. The broadcast reports winds 240 at 15 knots, visibility 2 miles in mist, and a temperature/dewpoint spread of 1 degree. This objective data immediately alters decision-making. The visibility is below VFR minimums for Class E airspace, and the narrow temperature spread indicates imminent fog. The pilot aborts the visual approach and diverts to an alternate airport inland.

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Ignoring Density Altitude:** Listening to the AWOS and noting the temperature is 35°C (95°F) but failing to apply that data to the aircraft's performance charts, resulting in a dangerously long takeoff roll or an inability to climb out of the traffic pattern.
* **Old ATIS Information:** Acknowledging "Information Charlie" to the tower when "Information Delta" was just released because the wind shifted 40 degrees, leading to an unexpected crosswind or tailwind clearance.
