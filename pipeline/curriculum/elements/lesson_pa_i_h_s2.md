## PA.I.H.S2: Perform self-assessment, including fitness for flight and personal minimums

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
The applicant must demonstrate the ability to honestly and objectively evaluate their physical and mental state using the IMSAFE checklist, and measure the flight's overall risk using the PAVE checklist. Furthermore, the pilot must articulate strict, pre-established written personal minimums that provide a safety buffer exceeding FAA baseline legal minimums.

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 61.53 (Medical deficiency); 14 CFR 91.103 (Preflight action).

**The "Why":** Legal currency does not equal operational proficiency. The FAA regulations (e.g., allowing VFR flight with only 3 statute miles of visibility) are absolute baselines designed for highly proficient, current pilots operating optimal aircraft. Human factors dictate that a pilot's capability degrades rapidly with stress, fatigue, or time away from the cockpit. Establishing written personal minimums removes the dangerous emotional pressure of the "Go/No-Go" decision at the airport. If a pilot establishes *in writing* at home that they will not fly in crosswinds greater than 10 knots, they do not have to debate their ego or succumb to peer pressure when the ATIS reports 15 knots on the ramp.

**Scenario Application:** The evaluator provides a scenario: "It has been 60 days since your last flight. You are legally current to carry passengers. The weather is reporting 4 miles visibility with scattered showers." The applicant must apply the PAVE checklist. P (Pilot): Legally current, but out of practice (low proficiency). V (Environment): Marginal VFR weather. The applicant demonstrates proper risk mitigation by stating: "My written personal minimums require at least 6 miles visibility if I haven't flown in the last 30 days to account for my degraded proficiency. Therefore, my self-assessment dictates this is a No-Go decision, and I will cancel the flight."

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Treating Minimums as Flexible:** A pilot who alters their personal minimums on the ramp to justify making the flight completely defeats the purpose of proactive risk management. Minimums must be treated as hard limits.
* **Conflating IMSAFE and PAVE:** IMSAFE is strictly for evaluating the *Pilot's* physiological body and mind. PAVE is the macro-level tool used to evaluate the *entire* flight system (Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External Pressures).
