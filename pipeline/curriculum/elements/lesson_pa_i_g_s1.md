## PA.I.G.S1: Operate at least three of the systems appropriately

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
The applicant must physically demonstrate the correct normal operation, identify the operational limitations, and explain the practical application of at least three aircraft systems (such as the electrical, environmental, and fuel systems) directly in accordance with the manufacturer's approved procedures in the actual aircraft.

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")

**Regulatory Basis:** Practical Test Standards require the safe, competent physical operation of the airplane systems provided for the flight test, ensuring the pilot's theoretical ground knowledge translates directly into physical cockpit competency.

**The "Why":** Theoretical systems knowledge is operationally useless if a pilot cannot physically locate the specific switch, circuit breaker, or control lever in the actual aircraft during a high-stress environment. Operating the systems appropriately means demonstrating a mastery of "switchology," understanding the exact sequence of engagement, and knowing the physical limitations of the hardware. For example, demonstrating the fuel system involves correctly utilizing the fuel selector valve to switch tanks in flight, verifying positive fuel pressure, and utilizing the auxiliary electric pump precisely as dictated by the POH. Demonstrating the environmental system requires safely engaging the exhaust shroud cabin heat while articulating the mitigation strategies and emergency procedures for carbon monoxide exposure.

**Scenario Application:** During the practical test, the examiner asks you to demonstrate the operation of the electrical system. You correctly articulate the voltage difference between the battery and the alternator, explain the function of the voltage regulator, and then physically demonstrate how to isolate the alternator by splitting the master switch. You point to the ammeter to verify the resulting electrical discharge, explain the necessity of load shedding, and then successfully bring the alternator back online, verifying the positive charge.

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Rote Memorization without Location:** A candidate knowing exactly what an alternate static system does theoretically, but freezing and failing the task when asked by the examiner to physically point to the alternate static valve under the instrument panel in the actual airplane.
* **Operating Outside Limitations:** Activating the electrical pitot heat on the ground for an extended period during a system demonstration. Without the cooling effect of high-speed ram air, the heating element will rapidly overheat and burn out, destroying the system.
