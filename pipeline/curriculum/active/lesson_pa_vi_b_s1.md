## PA.VI.B.S1: Use an airborne electronic navigation system

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
[cite_start]To practically demonstrate the use of an airborne electronic navigation system, the applicant must smoothly and accurately operate the specific avionics installed in the aircraft (or an approved portable device). [cite: 1618] [cite_start]This involves physically powering the unit, selecting the appropriate VOR frequency or programming the correct GPS waypoint, positively identifying the station via Morse code or database validation, and configuring the Course Deviation Indicator (CDI) or Horizontal Situation Indicator (HSI) to display the required navigation data for the flight. [cite: 1619]

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")

#### A. The "Tune, Identify, Twist" Flow
[cite_start]The mechanical operation of a ground-based navigation receiver (VOR) relies on a highly standardized physical flow to prevent critical errors. [cite: 1621]
* [cite_start]**Tune:** The pilot dials the correct VHF frequency into the standby side of the NAV radio and swaps it into the active window. [cite: 1623]
* [cite_start]**Identify:** This is the most critical and frequently failed step on practical tests. [cite: 1624] [cite_start]The pilot must pull the NAV volume knob and actively listen to the Morse Code identifier. [cite: 1625] [cite_start]If the identifier is absent or incorrect, the signal is illegal to use for navigation, regardless of what the needle indicates. [cite: 1626]
* [cite_start]**Twist:** The pilot turns the Omni Bearing Selector (OBS) to align the desired magnetic course with the top index, bringing the CDI needle into view. [cite: 1627]

#### B. GPS Programming and Sequencing
[cite_start]For satellite-based systems, the physical flow differs substantially. [cite: 1629] [cite_start]The pilot must power up the unit and acknowledge the database currency and RAIM predictions. [cite: 1629] [cite_start]To navigate, the pilot utilizes the "Direct-To" or "Flight Plan" functions. [cite: 1630] [cite_start]The critical skill here involves verifying the specific waypoint. [cite: 1630] [cite_start]When a pilot types in "KORD", they must verify the spelled-out name "CHICAGO OHARE INTL" on the screen before executing the command. [cite: 1631] [cite_start]Failure to do so leads to Garbage In, Garbage Out. [cite: 1632] [cite_start]Furthermore, the pilot must explicitly ensure the GPS is in the correct CDI mode (e.g., VLOC vs. GPS on a Garmin unit) to ensure the panel instrument is actually reading the satellite data. [cite: 1633]

#### C. Equipment Mandates for the Practical Test
[cite_start]An FAA InFO (17003) clarified that the practical test requires the use of an onboard electronic navigation system. [cite: 1634] [cite_start]If the aircraft is a vintage Piper Cub with no electrical system, the applicant must accomplish this task using a handheld GPS or EFB. [cite: 1635] [cite_start]The inability to demonstrate electronic navigation constitutes an automatic failure to meet the ACS standards, as this foundational skill cannot be waived by the examiner. [cite: 1636]

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* [cite_start]**The VLOC/GPS Button:** On standard Garmin 430/530 or G1000 units, the CDI source button toggles the needle between ground-based (VLOC) and satellite-based (GPS) data. [cite: 1638] [cite_start]A common checkride failure occurs when a pilot programs a GPS waypoint but leaves the CDI slaved to VLOC; [cite: 1639] [cite_start]they chase a dead or irrelevant needle. [cite: 1640]
* [cite_start]**Skipping the Audio Identifier:** Tuning a VOR and blindly trusting the needle without taking the time to listen to the Morse code, violating basic signal integrity checks. [cite: 1641]
* [cite_start]**Button Mashing:** Becoming flustered with a complex GPS interface (like a GTN 750) and rapidly pressing "Clear" or "Direct", inadvertently erasing the active flight plan while the aircraft deviates from its heading. [cite: 1642]
