### PA.III.A.K3: ATC light signal recognition

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
ATC light gun signals are a backup communication method used by a control tower when an aircraft experiences a radio failure. The pilot must memorize the meanings: Steady Green (Cleared to land/takeoff), Flashing Green (Return for landing/Cleared to taxi), Steady Red (Give way and continue circling/Stop), Flashing Red (Airport unsafe/Taxi clear of runway), Flashing White (Return to starting point on airport), and Alternating Red/Green (Exercise extreme caution).

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 91.125 (ATC light signals).

**The "Why":** The light gun is a highly directional, intensely focused beam of light that a tower controller aims directly at a specific aircraft through a specialized sight. This backup system is physically robust, immune to electromagnetic interference, and bridges the gap when the aircraft's electrical system completely fails. The distinction between airborne and ground meanings is logically tied to the kinetic state of the aircraft. For instance, a "Steady Red" on the ground demands a cessation of kinetic energy (Stop), while in the air it demands a safe management of kinetic energy (Give way to other aircraft and continue circling). The pilot acknowledges airborne signals by rocking the wings in daylight or flashing navigational/landing lights at night.

**ATC Light Gun Signals Table:**

| Color and Type of Signal | Aircraft on the Ground | Aircraft in Flight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Steady Green** | Cleared for takeoff | Cleared to land |
| **Flashing Green** | Cleared to taxi | Return for landing |
| **Steady Red** | Stop | Give way and continue circling |
| **Flashing Red** | Taxi clear of runway in use | Airport unsafe—do not land |
| **Flashing White** | Return to starting point on airport | Not applicable |
| **Alternating Red/Green** | Exercise extreme caution | Exercise extreme caution |

**Scenario Application:** A pilot is five miles out from a Class D airport when the avionics stack goes completely dark due to an electrical fire. The pilot continues the approach while maintaining a rigorous visual scan. Entering the downwind leg, the pilot looks at the control tower cab and observes a brilliant, steady green light tracking the aircraft. The pilot rocks the wings to acknowledge, proceeds to land, and exits the runway.

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The Flashing Green Confusion:** Pilots often forget that a Flashing Green light in flight means "Return for landing," which is not a clearance to land. It must be followed by a Steady Green light at the proper time to legally touch down.
* **Failure to Acknowledge:** Pilots receiving a light signal often stare at it without rocking their wings, leaving the controller unsure if the instruction was received, which can lead to escalating emergency responses.
