## PA.VI.B.S4: Recognize and describe the indication of station or waypoint passage

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
[cite_start]Station passage over a VOR is exclusively confirmed by the first positive, complete reversal of the TO/FROM indicator (from TO to FROM). [cite: 1722] [cite_start]Waypoint passage on a GPS is indicated by the sequencing of the active waypoint data block to the next waypoint in the flight plan, often accompanied by a message flashing and the CDI auto-slewing to the new course. [cite: 1723] [cite_start]The pilot must accurately describe these indications and not be tricked by hypersensitive needle oscillations prior to passage. [cite: 1724]

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")

#### A. VOR Station Passage and the Cone of Confusion
[cite_start]As an aircraft approaches a VOR station, the navigation signal travels almost vertically up to the belly of the aircraft rather than horizontally. [cite: 1725] [cite_start]This creates an inverted cone-shaped area directly above the antenna known as the Cone of Confusion. [cite: 1726] [cite_start]When the aircraft enters this cone, the receiver cannot accurately determine the phase difference. [cite: 1726] [cite_start]The CDI needle will become hypersensitive and begin to swing wildly from side to side, and the TO/FROM flag may briefly disappear or flicker into the OFF/NAV warning position. [cite: 1727] [cite_start]The pilot must hold a constant magnetic heading through this unstable zone. [cite: 1728] [cite_start]True station passage has only occurred when the aircraft exits the other side of the cone and the flag firmly snaps from TO to FROM. [cite: 1729]

#### B. GPS Waypoint Sequencing Dynamics
[cite_start]GPS waypoint passage is vastly more precise because it relies on digital geometry rather than analog radio limits. [cite: 1731] [cite_start]As the aircraft approaches a waypoint, the GPS receiver calculates the exact turn radius required to capture the next leg of the flight plan based on the aircraft's current groundspeed. [cite: 1732]
* [cite_start]**Turn Anticipation:** The GPS will generally provide a visual alert (e.g., "NEXT DTK 090 in 10 sec") before reaching the physical point, prompting the pilot to begin the turn early to roll out on course. [cite: 1733]
* [cite_start]**Sequencing:** Actual passage occurs when the GPS automatically drops the current waypoint and activates the next leg, slewing the CDI needle to the new course. [cite: 1734]

#### C. The Practical Test Standard
[cite_start]To successfully meet the ACS standard, the applicant must verbally call out station passage to the evaluator. [cite: 1736] [cite_start]A premature callout based on needle movement rather than flag reversal demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of VOR physics and is grounds for failure. [cite: 1737]

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* [cite_start]**Chasing the Needle in the Cone:** As the needle pegs to full deflection inside the cone of confusion, the untrained pilot banks sharply to catch it, completely destabilizing the aircraft right as it passes the station. [cite: 1739]
* [cite_start]**Premature Turn on a VOR:** Executing the turn to the next airway heading before the TO/FROM flag flips, resulting in a failure to properly intercept the outbound radial. [cite: 1740]
* [cite_start]**Manual Sequencing Failure:** In older GPS units, or when a unit is placed in "OBS/SUSP" mode, the GPS will not auto-sequence to the next waypoint. [cite: 1741] [cite_start]The pilot crosses the fix, but the system continues providing guidance away from the waypoint endlessly until manually unsuspended. [cite: 1742]
