## PA.VI.B.S3: Intercept and track a given course, radial, or bearing

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
[cite_start]Intercepting a course requires the pilot to turn the aircraft to a definitive intercept heading (typically 30 to 45 degrees into the desired course) and maintain that heading until the CDI needle begins to center. [cite: 1684] [cite_start]Tracking the course requires establishing an exact Wind Correction Angle (WCA) to counteract crosswinds, keeping the needle centered. [cite: 1685] [cite_start]The applicant must demonstrate the ability to smoothly bracket the heading and maintain the selected altitude (±200 feet) and heading (±15°) throughout the entire maneuver. [cite: 1686]

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")

#### A. The Interception Geometry
[cite_start]To intercept a given VOR radial or GPS course, the pilot must conceptualize the geometric relationship of the intercept. [cite: 1689] [cite_start]First, twist the OBS to the desired course (e.g., 360°). [cite: 1690] [cite_start]If the CDI needle deflects to the left, the course lies physically to the left of the aircraft. [cite: 1691] [cite_start]The pilot determines an intercept angle. [cite: 1692] [cite_start]For a VFR intercept, a 30-to-45 degree angle is standard. [cite: 1692] [cite_start]The pilot turns to a heading of 330° and rigidly holds it. [cite: 1693] [cite_start]As the aircraft approaches the course, the needle will "come alive" and move toward the center. [cite: 1694] [cite_start]The pilot leads the turn to roll out on the 360° heading exactly as the needle centers, avoiding sloppy overshoots. [cite: 1695]

#### B. Tracking and Bracketing Aerodynamics
[cite_start]Once established on the course, crosswinds will immediately begin to drift the aircraft. [cite: 1697] [cite_start]If the needle begins drifting left, the wind is pushing the aircraft to the right. [cite: 1698] [cite_start]The pilot must employ the Bracketing technique: [cite: 1699]
1. [cite_start]Turn 10 degrees left (into the wind) to arrest the drift and re-intercept the course. [cite: 1700]
2. [cite_start]Once the needle centers, take out half the correction (turn 5 degrees right). [cite: 1701]
3. Hold this new heading. [cite_start]If the needle stays centered, the pilot has successfully found the exact Wind Correction Angle for the current atmospheric conditions. [cite: 1702]

#### C. Managing System Sensitivity
[cite_start]The physical mechanics of tracking change based on the system. [cite: 1704] [cite_start]A VOR signal is angular. [cite: 1704] [cite_start]As the aircraft gets closer to the transmitting station, the radials converge, making the CDI needle exponentially more sensitive. [cite: 1705] [cite_start]A 1-degree heading change close to the VOR will cause massive needle swings. [cite: 1706] [cite_start]Conversely, standard en-route GPS provides linear sensitivity (e.g., exactly 2 NM full-scale deflection) regardless of distance to the waypoint. [cite: 1707] [cite_start]The pilot must adjust their control inputs accordingly. [cite: 1708]

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* [cite_start]**Chasing the Needle:** The most common error in instrument tracking. [cite: 1710] [cite_start]Instead of holding a heading and watching the needle trend, the pilot continuously yanks the yoke left and right in synchronization with the CDI needle's movements, resulting in a dangerous, serpentine flight path. [cite: 1711]
* [cite_start]**Turning the Wrong Way:** If the pilot sets a course on the OBS but the aircraft is physically flying the opposite direction, the needle will exhibit reverse sensing. [cite: 1712] [cite_start]The pilot will instinctively turn toward the needle, driving the aircraft further away from the intended course. [cite: 1713]
* [cite_start]**Overcorrecting near the Station:** Making 20-degree heading corrections when 2 miles from a VOR station. [cite: 1714] [cite_start]The extreme sensitivity of the converging radials guarantees an immediate, aggressive overshoot. [cite: 1715]
