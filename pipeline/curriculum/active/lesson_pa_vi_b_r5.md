## PA.VI.B.R5: Use of an electronic flight bag (EFB), if used

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
[cite_start]Electronic Flight Bags (EFBs), such as iPads running ForeFlight or Garmin Pilot, offer unparalleled situational awareness but introduce unique technological risks, including rapid battery depletion, thermal shutdown, and software crashes. [cite: 1590] [cite_start]Under Part 91, EFBs are perfectly legal to use as a total replacement for paper charts provided they display functionally equivalent information. [cite: 1591] [cite_start]To effectively mitigate EFB failure, pilots must employ strict battery management, keep the device out of direct sunlight, and always carry a reliable backup—such as a secondary device, a panel-mounted display, or printed paper charts. [cite: 1592]

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")

#### A. The Regulatory Standard and AC 91-78A
[cite_start]The FAA fundamentally altered the modern cockpit environment with the issuance and updates of Advisory Circular 91-78A. [cite: 1593] [cite_start]This critical document provides the explicit legal authorization for Part 91 operators to completely remove paper aeronautical charts from the cockpit. [cite: 1594] [cite_start]The AC stipulates that a Class 1 (portable) or Class 2 (mounted) EFB can replace paper provided the digital information is functionally equivalent to the paper reference material and the database is kept current. [cite: 1594] [cite_start]Notably, the AC is permissive; it does not legally mandate a secondary backup for Part 91 operators, but it heavily suggests one as a fundamental best practice. [cite: 1595]

#### B. Hardware Vulnerabilities (Thermal & Power)
[cite_start]The most frequent cause of EFB failure in general aviation is not software bugs, but severe physical environmental stress. [cite: 1597]
* [cite_start]**Thermal Shutdown:** Consumer tablets like iPads are not built to withstand the extreme greenhouse effect of an aircraft canopy. [cite: 1598] [cite_start]Placing an EFB on the glare shield in direct sunlight will rapidly induce a critical thermal shutdown, rendering the device an expensive paperweight until it physically cools. [cite: 1599]
* [cite_start]**Power Management:** An EFB running a bright screen and continuous GPS tracking consumes battery at a massive rate. [cite: 1600] [cite_start]Pilots must ensure the device starts at 100% and should utilize an external power bank or certified aircraft USB port to maintain charge. [cite: 1601]
* [cite_start]**RF Interference:** Unnecessary radios (Cellular/Bluetooth) should be disabled or placed in "Airplane Mode" to prevent RF interference with the aircraft's panel-mounted navigation equipment. [cite: 1602]

#### C. The EFB Fallback Doctrine
[cite_start]Rigorous risk management requires a comprehensive "Plan B" if the primary iPad suddenly fails. [cite: 1603]

| Fallback Strategy | Description | Practical Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Device Redundancy** | Carrying a secondary PED. | [cite_start]A smartphone running the same EFB software, fully charged and accessible. [cite: 1604] |
| **Panel Integration** | Utilizing certified avionics. | [cite_start]Relying on the MFD of a Garmin G1000 for approach plates and navigation. [cite: 1604] |
| **Paper Backup** | The ultimate fail-safe. | [cite_start]Printing departure, destination, and alternate airport diagrams prior to flight. [cite: 1604] |

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* [cite_start]**The "Update in the Cockpit" Trap:** Attempting to download massive chart database updates over a weak cellular connection while holding short of the runway. [cite: 1606] [cite_start]If the update fails, hangs, or corrupts, the entire EFB database may become inaccessible for the flight. [cite: 1607]
* [cite_start]**Target Fixation:** Treating the EFB as a primary flight display. [cite: 1608] [cite_start]Pilots will stare at the synthesized vision or attitude indicator on their iPad instead of looking out the window for VFR traffic or scanning their certified panel instruments. [cite: 1609]
* [cite_start]**Mounting Hazards:** Using a cheap, unrated suction cup mount that fails during turbulence, sending the heavy tablet crashing into the flight controls or the pilot's knees. [cite: 1610] [cite_start]AC 91-78A explicitly requires that mounted EFBs do not interfere with flight control movement. [cite: 1611]
