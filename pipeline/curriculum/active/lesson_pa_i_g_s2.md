## PA.I.G.S2: Complete the appropriate checklist(s)

### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
The applicant must consistently utilize the approved manufacturer's checklist for all phases of flight and system operations, actively selecting the proper execution method (read-and-do for normal ground operations, or do-and-verify for high-workload flight phases) to ensure no critical items or limitations are missed.

### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")

**Regulatory Basis:** The Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3C) explicitly emphasizes that the adherence to and proper use of the AFM/POH and checklists is the primary method to ensure the airplane is in a safe condition for flight and complies with 14 CFR part 91.

**The "Why":** Aviation environments are fundamentally unforgiving of human memory lapses. Checklists act as the primary, physical defense against human fallibility, distraction, and omission. There are two primary, distinct methodologies for running a checklist.
* The **"Read-and-do"** method is used when time is plentiful and the aircraft is stationary (e.g., preflight inspection or engine run-up); the pilot reads the item aloud and then physically executes the action.
* The **"Do-and-verify"** method is strictly used during high-workload, dynamic phases of flight (e.g., after-takeoff, entering the traffic pattern, or emergency procedures); the pilot executes a logical physical flow pattern around the cockpit from memory to configure the aircraft rapidly, and then subsequently pulls out the physical checklist to quickly verify that no critical items were missed.

**Scenario Application:** During an unannounced emergency engine failure simulation on the checkride, instead of immediately pulling out the physical checklist and reading it while the aircraft loses altitude, you instantly execute your memory flow items (Pitch for Vg, select a landing field, switch fuel tanks, mixture rich, fuel pump on). Once the aircraft is stabilized and gliding safely toward the selected field, you then pull out the physical emergency checklist to *verify* you completed all the necessary restart steps.

### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **The "Pencil Whip":** Reading the checklist items aloud rapidly while your hands do nothing, completely failing to actually look at, touch, or verify the position of the associated switches and levers.
* **Dangerous Checklist Dependency:** Keeping your head down inside the cockpit during taxiing or flight to meticulously read a checklist, thereby compromising situational awareness, drifting off the centerline, and failing to clear the outside airspace for traffic.
