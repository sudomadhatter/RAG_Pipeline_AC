### PA.III.A.K5: Lost communication procedures

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
If a pilot loses two-way radio communications under VFR, they should first troubleshoot the equipment (volume, squelch, headsets, alternate frequency). If communication cannot be restored, the pilot must squawk 7600 on the transponder to alert ATC. The pilot should remain outside or above controlled airspace if possible, determine the traffic flow at the destination, enter the pattern safely, and watch the control tower for light gun signals.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 91.185 (IFR operations: Two-way radio communications failure - specifically the VFR clause in paragraph b) and AIM 4-2-13.

**The "Why":** Complete radio failure isolates the pilot from the traffic management network. Squawking 7600 changes the aircraft's data tag on the ATC radar scope to flash "RDOF" (Radio Failure), instantly notifying controllers to clear airspace ahead of the aircraft. 14 CFR 91.185(b) explicitly dictates that if the failure occurs in VFR conditions, or if VFR conditions are encountered after the failure, the pilot shall continue the flight under VFR and land as soon as practicable. This physical decoupling from the IFR/ATC system ensures the aircraft becomes predictable. A predictable aircraft allows controllers to vector other traffic away from the "deaf" aircraft while preparing the tower for light gun signaling.

**Scenario Application:** An aircraft is operating in Class E airspace when a frayed wire shorts the audio panel. After checking connections and trying the handheld radio with no success, the pilot squawks 7600. Bound for a Class D airport, the pilot remains at 500 feet above the traffic pattern altitude to observe the active runway and traffic flow. The pilot sequences smoothly into the downwind leg, looking at the tower, and lands upon receiving a steady green light.

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Immediate Panic:** Treating a lost comm as an absolute life-or-death emergency requiring an off-field landing. Unless in hard IFR, a VFR lost comm is an inconvenience, not a disaster. Fly the airplane first.
* **Forgetting the Cell Phone:** In the modern era, if the aircraft is close enough to the ground or cell towers, simply connecting a Bluetooth headset to a smartphone and calling the Tower/TRACON phone number is an entirely legal and highly effective backup.
