### PA.VII.D.K2: Causes of a spin and the phases of a spin.

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
The root cause of a spin is the pilot exceeding the critical angle of attack while the aircraft is uncoordinated (yawing). A spin develops through four distinct sequential phases: (1) The Entry phase, where the pro-spin inputs are applied; (2) The Incipient phase, consisting of the first 1-2 turns as rotation accelerates; (3) The Developed phase, where aerodynamic and inertial forces reach a stabilized steady state; and (4) The Recovery phase, where anti-spin control inputs break the autorotation.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** Evaluated under the FAA-S-ACS-6C Task D, requiring theoretical knowledge for Private Pilots, as actual spin training is reserved for CFI candidates.

**The "Why":** Understanding the phases dictates the urgency of recovery. An incipient spin is highly recoverable; a fully developed spin requires precise, deliberate inputs and time to arrest.

**Scenario Application:** Analyzing flight data recorder telemetry of an aircraft that entered an uncoordinated stall, tumbled through an incipient phase, and locked into a 4,000 FPM vertical descent.

**The Root Cause: Uncoordinated Stalls**
While the stall is the aerodynamic prerequisite, yaw is the physical catalyst. Uncoordinated flight can be introduced via multiple vectors in the cockpit:
* **Adverse Yaw:** Deflecting the ailerons at slow speeds creates massive induced drag on the up-wing. If uncorrected by rudder, this pulls the nose away from the turn direction.
* **Engine Torque/P-Factor:** At high power and low airspeed (e.g., during a departure stall), the physics of the spinning propeller forcibly yaw the aircraft to the left. If the pilot has lazy feet, the aircraft is uncoordinated.
* **Pilot Error (The Skid):** The pilot deliberately applying too much rudder into a turn to increase the turn rate without increasing bank angle.

If the critical AOA is crossed while any of these yawing moments are active, the aircraft will immediately snap into a spin.

**The Four Phases of a Spin (AC 61-67C)**
Advisory Circular 61-67C exhaustively details the anatomy of the spin, breaking it into four distinct aerodynamic epochs.
1.  **The Entry Phase:** This is the period where the pilot intentionally or accidentally introduces the pro-spin elements: pitching the aircraft up to the stall threshold while applying or failing to prevent yaw.
2.  **The Incipient Phase:** This is the transitional, chaotic period spanning from the exact moment the aircraft stalls and rotation begins until the spin fully stabilizes. During the incipient phase (typically lasting the first 2 to 4 seconds, or 1 to 2 complete turns), the aerodynamic forces (lift/drag) and inertial forces (centrifugal/gyroscopic) are fighting for dominance. The pitch attitude is rapidly changing, the flight path is erratic, and the rotation rate is accelerating. This is the optimal time for recovery, as the autorotation is not yet "locked in," and simply neutralizing the controls will often recover the aircraft.
3.  **The Developed Phase:** The spin matures into a steady-state condition. The aerodynamic forces and inertial forces are now perfectly balanced. The aircraft descends in a nearly vertical, helical path. The rotation rate, pitch attitude, vertical speed (often exceeding 4,000 to 6,000 feet per minute), and indicated airspeed are completely stabilized from turn to turn.
4.  **The Recovery Phase:** This phase begins the absolute moment the pilot applies proper anti-spin flight control inputs (e.g., PARE) and ends when the aircraft returns to normal, unstalled, level flight. Depending on the aircraft design, mass distribution, and how deeply developed the spin became, recovery can take anywhere from a quarter of a turn to several full rotations.

| Spin Phase | Duration / Turns | Aerodynamic State | Recommended Pilot Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Entry** | Pre-Stall | High AOA, Yawing | Lower AOA, Coordinate Rudder |
| **Incipient** | 1-2 Turns | Chaotic, Accelerating | Neutralize Controls / PARE |
| **Developed** | Steady State | Balanced Forces, Fast Sink | Execute PARE Procedure |
| **Recovery** | 0.5 to 2 Turns | Breaking Autorotation | Neutralize Rudder, Pull Out |

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Airspeed Misinterpretation:** Believing that because the nose is pointed straight down in the developed phase, the aircraft is accelerating toward $V_{ne}$. The massive drag of the stalled wings acts like a parachute, freezing the indicated airspeed at a very low value.
* **Skipping the Incipient Phase:** Failing to recognize that a rapid, immediate recovery at the incipient stage prevents the violent, disorienting stabilization of the developed phase, saving hundreds of feet of altitude.
