### PA.VII.D.K3: Spin recovery procedure.

#### 1. The Oral Standard (The Direct Answer)
If an inadvertent spin occurs, the pilot must immediately execute the NASA standard spin recovery procedure, summarized by the acronym PARE: (P) Power to idle, (A) Ailerons neutral, (R) Rudder full opposite to the direction of rotation, and (E) Elevator forward to break the stall. Once rotation stops, the pilot must neutralize the rudder and smoothly recover from the resulting dive. Always defer to the manufacturer's specific POH procedures if they differ from PARE.

#### 2. The Expert Deep Dive (The "Textbook")
**Regulatory Basis:** 14 CFR 91.3 requires the pilot to act decisively to maintain safety.

**The "Why":** The PARE sequence systematically dismantles the aerodynamic feedback loop of autorotation.

**Scenario Application:** A pilot inadvertently enters a departure spin to the left. They methodically chop power, center the yoke, stomp the right rudder pedal, and push forward on the elevator to arrest the rotation.

**The "PARE" Mechanism Explained**
The PARE acronym is a sequenced, anti-spin algorithm developed through extensive NASA and FAA flight testing to aggressively dismantle the aerodynamic forces sustaining the autorotation. Each step serves a critical, physics-based function. Spin recoveries are procedures; they do not reward panic or speed, they reward exact sequence.

* **P - Power to Idle:** In a spin, engine power is the enemy. The propeller slipstream accelerates air over the horizontal stabilizer, which artificially increases the downward aerodynamic force on the tail. This pushes the nose higher and "flattens" the spin. A flat spin is often fatal because the rudder becomes completely blanked by the wake of the fuselage and stalled wing, removing the pilot's ability to stop the yaw. Furthermore, the gyroscopic precession of the spinning propeller can accelerate the spin rate. Cutting power removes these pro-spin forces immediately.
* **A - Ailerons Neutral:** A pilot's natural, untrained instinct when inverted or spinning is to "roll" out using ailerons. This is a fatal impulse. If spinning to the left, applying right aileron physically lowers the aileron surface on the left wing. This effectively increases the camber and the Angle of Attack on the left wing, deepening the stall on the already dominant pro-spin wing. Ailerons must be absolutely neutral.
* **R - Rudder Full Opposite:** The rudder is the primary tool to kill the yawing moment. The pilot must apply full, physical force to the rudder pedal opposite the direction of rotation (e.g., full right rudder for a left spin). This creates a massive aerodynamic anti-spin torque that halts the autorotation.
* **E - Elevator Briskly Forward:** Once the yaw is arrested, the wings are still stalled. Pushing the elevator forward (briskly, to neutral or slightly past) decreases the pitch attitude, reducing the AOA below the critical threshold. This breaks the stall, reattaching the boundary layer to both wings simultaneously.

**The Aftermath: The Pull-Out**
Once PARE is executed, the rotation will stop. At this precise moment, the pilot must immediately neutralize the rudder. If they hold the anti-spin rudder too long after rotation stops, the aircraft will violently snap into a crossover spin in the opposite direction. With rotation stopped and wings un-stalled, the aircraft is now in a steep, accelerating dive. The pilot must apply smooth, steady back pressure to recover to level flight. Pulling too aggressively or abruptly will trigger an accelerated secondary stall, putting the aircraft right back into danger.

#### 3. Common Errors & Gotchas
* **Using Ailerons:** The most common and lethal mistake in spin recovery is attempting to fight the spin with the yoke (ailerons), which only aggravates the autorotation.
* **Slow or Timid Inputs:** A spin is a high-energy aerodynamic event. Flight control inputs for recovery must be brisk, absolute, and full-deflection. Gently feeding in opposite rudder will simply not break the autorotation of a developed spin.
* **POH Deviations:** While PARE is the gold standard, some specific aircraft have unique design quirks. For example, some POHs explicitly mandate the immediate retraction of flaps, as deployed flaps can alter the airflow over the empennage during the spin, delaying recovery. Always follow the AFM/POH as the final authority.
