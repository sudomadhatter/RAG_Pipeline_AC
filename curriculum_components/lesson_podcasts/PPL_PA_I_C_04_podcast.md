# Lesson 13: PIREPs, AIRMETs, SIGMETs, and Convective SIGMETs
## PPL_PA_I_C_04 | ACS Elements: PA.I.C.K2d, PA.I.C.K2f, PA.I.C.K2g

### What This Lesson Covers
These are the products that tell you whether the atmosphere will hurt or kill you. PIREPs tell you what is actually happening in the air. AIRMETs warn of moderate hazards for light aircraft. SIGMETs warn of severe hazards for all aircraft. Convective SIGMETs are specifically for convective weather. The Convective Outlook tells you what the next four days look like for storm risk.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: PIREPs — The Only Ground Truth Product**
Every other weather product is a forecast or model output. PIREPs are actual pilot reports of actual conditions. Two types: UA (routine) and UUA (urgent). File a UUA for: tornadoes, funnel clouds, severe or extreme turbulence, severe icing, hail at the surface. You are not legally required to file a PIREP — but failing to file a UUA after a severe encounter is professionally unacceptable.

Key fields: /OV (location), /TM (Zulu time), /FL (altitude), /TP (aircraft type), /SK (sky/cloud), /WX (visibility), /TA (temp), /WV (wind), /TB (turbulence), /IC (icing), /RM (remarks).

Turbulence codes: NEG / LGT / MOD / SEV / EXTRM.
Icing codes: TRACE / LGT / MOD / SEV.

**RKP_02: AIRMETs — Sierra, Tango, Zulu (6-Hour Validity)**
Target: light aircraft primarily. Valid 6 hours. Three types to name by letter AND content:
- Sierra (S): IFR conditions (ceiling below 1,000 AGL / visibility below 3SM affecting 50%+ of area) OR mountain obscuration
- Tango (T): Moderate turbulence, sustained surface winds 30+ knots, non-convective LLWS below 2,000 AGL
- Zulu (Z): Moderate icing + freezing level altitudes

AIRMETs = MODERATE-level hazards. SIGMETs = SEVERE/EXTREME. Know the cut line.

G-AIRMETs are graphical versions — higher resolution, issued every 3 hours.

**RKP_03: SIGMETs vs. Convective SIGMETs**
SIGMET (WS) — all aircraft — 4-hour validity (6 hours for hurricanes):
Severe/extreme turbulence (not convective), severe icing (not convective), widespread dust/sandstorm below 3SM visibility, volcanic ash.

Convective SIGMET (WST) — all aircraft — 2-hour validity:
Severe thunderstorms (50+ knot surface wind, 3/4"+ hail, tornadoes), embedded TSTMs, lines of storms 60+ nm, area coverage 40%+ over 3,000 sq mi.
Issued at C55 (55 minutes past the hour) and as specials. Does not require active storms at issuance time — the threat qualifies.

CWA (Center Weather Advisory): unscheduled, 2-hour validity, issued by CWSU when approaching SIGMET/Convective SIGMET criteria. The real-time early warning system.

**RKP_04: Convective Outlook — SPC Strategic Planning**
Issued by the Storm Prediction Center (SPC). Covers days 1, 2, 3, and 4–8. Risk categories: Marginal → Slight → Enhanced → Moderate → High. A Moderate or High risk area on your route the day before departure is a strategic flag for re-routing or go/no-go planning, long before any SIGMET is issued.

### Key Regulations
- 14 CFR 91.103 — Requires checking for known adverse weather including AIRMETs and SIGMETs
- AC 00-45H — Aviation Weather Services (primary inflight advisory decode reference)
- AIM 7-1-6 — Inflight Aviation Weather Advisories
