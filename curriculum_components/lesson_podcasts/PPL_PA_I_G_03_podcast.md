# Lesson 29: Fuel and Oil Systems — Types, Contamination, and Engine Cooling
## PPL_PA_I_G_03 | ACS Elements: PA.I.G.K1d, PA.I.G.K1e

**RKP_01: Fuel System Architecture**
High-wing (C172): gravity-feed — BOTH tanks, no boost pump required normally.
Low-wing (PA-28): pump-fed — engine-driven pump primary; electric boost pump ON for T/O and landing.
Vented tanks: clogged vent = vacuum lock = fuel starvation. Check vents on preflight.

**RKP_02: Contamination and Fuel Grades**
Water: clear bubbles below blue fuel. Drain until clear — if persists, GROUND the aircraft.
100LL Blue: standard GA piston. Mogas: only with STC authorization. Jet-A: NEVER in piston engine.
Wrong octane: detonation risk. Always verify fuel grade matches TCDS requirement.

**RKP_03: Engine Cooling and Shock Cooling**
Air-cooled engine: fins + baffled cowl airflow. Climb = highest heat, lowest ram cooling.
CHT normal range: 200-400°F (green arc). Exceeding the arc = detonation risk.
SHOCK COOLING: rapid power reduction → rapid CHT drop → thermal crack risk.
Prevention: reduce power gradually (max 50°F/min CHT change). Never idle-dive from cruise.

**RKP_04: Oil System and Low Oil Pressure Emergency**
Oil functions: lubricate, cool internal components, seal piston rings, clean deposits.
Normal pressure: 25-65 PSI cruise. Low oil pressure = EMERGENCY.
Action: reduce power immediately, declare emergency, divert, land ASAP. Engine may seize without warning.
Preflight check: verify oil level within POH range (typically 6-8 quart operating range).

### Key Regulations
- 14 CFR 91.7 — Airworthiness: fuel contamination or oil system anomaly grounds the aircraft
