# Lesson 5: Required Aircraft Documents (ARROW)
## PPL_PA_I_B_01 | ACS Elements: PA.I.B.K1, PA.I.B.S1

### What This Lesson Covers
ARROW is the acronym every PPL applicant must know cold: five documents required on every civil aircraft operating in the United States. This lesson covers each document's specific display requirement, expiration rules, and the gotchas that trip applicants during DPE oral exams and FAA ramp checks.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: ARROW — The Five Required Documents**
A. Airworthiness Certificate — FAA Form 8100-2. Must be displayed at the cabin entrance, legible to passengers. Does not expire on its face, but its legal authority requires continuous maintenance compliance.

R. Registration Certificate — AC Form 8050-3. Under 14 CFR 47.40, expires 7 years after the last day of the month it was issued. Must be on board.

R. Radio Station License — only required for international flights. For all domestic VFR operations, aircraft are licensed by rule under 47 CFR Part 87. You do not need a radio license to fly within the United States.

O. Operating Limitations — the serialized, FAA-approved Airplane Flight Manual. This is aircraft-specific, tied to the tail number and serial number. Not a generic Jeppesen manual. Not a commercially purchased Pilot's Operating Handbook. The real AFM with the FAA approval stamp in the Limitations section.

W. Weight and Balance — the current W&B data that reflects this specific aircraft's current configuration. Updated whenever equipment is added or removed.

**RKP_02: The Airworthiness Certificate Display Requirement**
14 CFR 91.203(b) requires the Airworthiness Certificate to be displayed at the cabin or cockpit entrance so it is legible to passengers and crew. Not in a binder on the back seat. Not in the glovebox. Not in the flight bag. At the entrance. Legible. The examiner will physically ask you to locate it during the checkride. Know exactly where it is in the aircraft you fly.

The Airworthiness Certificate has no expiration date printed on the face. But the DPE will ask: does it ever expire? The correct answer is: the physical document does not expire, but its legal authority is conditioned on two things remaining true. First, the aircraft must remain registered in the United States. Second, all maintenance must be performed in accordance with Parts 43 and 91. If the aircraft falls out of compliance — a missed Annual, an overdue AD — the certificate's authority is suspended. The document still exists, but it no longer authorizes flight.

**RKP_03: The Registration Certificate — Seven-Year Update**
The FAA changed the registration validity from 3 years to 7 years via 14 CFR 47.40, effective through a 2022 Direct Final Rule. If your study materials say 3 years, they are outdated. Know 7 years.

The expiration is calculated from the last day of the month of issuance. Certificate issued March 4, 2024 — expires March 31, 2031.

When an aircraft is sold, the existing registration is immediately suspended — even before the new owner has paperwork. The buyer submits AC Form 8050-1. The pink copy of that application is legal for domestic flight for up to 90 days while the permanent certificate is processed. A photocopy of the registration is NOT legal.

**RKP_04: The AFM is Serialized — the POH is Not**
The Airplane Flight Manual is the legal document under 14 CFR 91.9. It carries a specific serial number, a tail number, and an FAA approval stamp in Section 2 (Limitations). The Pilot's Operating Handbook from the manufacturer or a commercial aviation publisher is a training aid — it does not have the FAA approval stamp and is not the legal AFM.

Same concept applies to Weight and Balance: it must reflect the exact current configuration of this specific aircraft. A W&B from the same model aircraft is not valid.

### Key Regulations
- 14 CFR 91.203 (Required aircraft documents — display)
- 14 CFR 91.9 (Operating limitations)
- 14 CFR 47.40 (7-year registration expiration)
- 14 CFR 21.181(a) (Airworthiness Certificate duration conditions)
- 47 CFR Part 87 (Radio Station License — international flights only)
