# Lesson 10: Weather Sources & Preflight Briefing Requirements
## PPL_PA_I_C_01 | ACS Elements: PA.I.C.K1, PA.I.C.S1

### What This Lesson Covers
Before you touch a weather product, you need to understand the regulatory basis for why you are required to brief at all — 14 CFR 91.103. Then you need to know which sources are authoritative, which briefing type to request, and why consumer apps are a fatal trap.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: 14 CFR 91.103 — The Law Behind the Briefing**
The entire weather briefing requirement flows from one regulation: 14 CFR 91.103, Preflight Action. For any flight not in the vicinity of an airport, it mandates weather reports and forecasts. 'All available information' is the operative language — not 'some information,' not 'a quick look.' The DPE will ask which regulation requires a preflight briefing. The answer is 91.103.

**RKP_02: Official Sources**
Three accepted categories:
1. Flight Service Station (FSS) — 1-800-WX-BRIEF or online at 1800wxbrief.com (Leidos). Provides human briefers for Standard, Abbreviated, and Outlook briefings. Note: DUATS was discontinued 2018. Do not mention DUATS.
2. AviationWeather.gov — direct NWS Aviation Weather Center portal. No commercial repackaging.
3. Approved EFBs (ForeFlight, Garmin Pilot, FlyQ) — approved under AC 91-78 as long as they source data from FAA/NWS feeds.

A consumer weather app does not meet any of these criteria.

**RKP_03: Three Briefing Types**
Standard Briefing: comprehensive, used when you have no prior briefing. Covers adverse conditions → synopsis → current conditions → en route forecasts → destination forecast → winds aloft → NOTAMs. This exact sequence matters — adverse conditions first.

Abbreviated Briefing: supplements or updates a prior briefing. 'Has the ceiling lifted since this morning?'

Outlook Briefing: departure 6+ hours in the future. Broad strategic planning only — not specific enough for actual flight decisions.

During the checkride oral, when asked what type of briefing you obtained, name the type and describe what it covered.

**RKP_04: Consumer Apps = Not Compliant**
A consumer app provides temperature and a rain icon. It does not provide: cloud bases in AGL, ceiling category (VFR/MVFR/IFR/LIFR), PIREPs, AIRMETs, SIGMETs, winds aloft at altitude, or NOTAMs. A pilot who uses a consumer app and then files a LIFR encounter has committed two violations simultaneously — 91.103 (inadequate briefing) and potentially 91.155 (below VFR minimums).

### Key Regulations
- 14 CFR 91.103 — Preflight action (the source of the weather briefing obligation)
- 14 CFR 91.155 — Basic VFR weather minimums
- AC 91-92 — Pilot's Guide to a Preflight Briefing
- AC 91-78 — EFB approval guidance
