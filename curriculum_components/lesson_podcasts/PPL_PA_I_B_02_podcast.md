# Lesson 6: Required Aircraft Inspections (AV1ATES)
## PPL_PA_I_B_02 | ACS Elements: PA.I.B.K1, PA.I.B.S2

### What This Lesson Covers
AV1ATES is the inspection acronym that complements ARROW. Together they cover what documents must be on the aircraft and what inspections must be current. This lesson focuses on the intervals, the who-can-perform-it rules, the 100-hour grace period math, and the 43.9 logbook entry requirements.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: AV1ATES — Seven Categories**
A — Annual: every 12 calendar months per 91.409(a).
V — VOR Check: every 30 days for IFR operations only per 91.171.
1 — 100-Hour: required only for commercial operations (for hire or dual instruction) per 91.409(b).
A — Altimeter/Pitot-Static: every 24 calendar months for IFR in controlled airspace per 91.411.
T — Transponder: every 24 calendar months for all operations requiring a transponder per 91.413.
E — ELT: every 12 calendar months for installation and battery check per 91.207. Battery must also be replaced when 50% of useful life has expired or after 1 cumulative hour of use.
S — Static System: covered under the Altimeter check.

**RKP_02: Annual vs. 100-Hour — Who Performs, When Required**
Annual inspection requires an Inspection Authorization holder — an IA-certificated mechanic. A standard A&P without an IA cannot sign off an Annual. No grace period at all. Expires on the last day of the 12th calendar month. If it expired yesterday, the aircraft is grounded and needs a Special Flight Permit to move to a maintenance facility.

100-Hour is a completely different rule. It is ONLY required when the aircraft is used commercially — either carrying a passenger for hire or provided for dual flight instruction. An A&P mechanic without IA designation can perform the 100-hour. For any personal VFR flight with no commercial purpose, the Annual is the only airframe-level inspection you need to track.

An Annual inspection satisfies the 100-hour requirement — it resets both clocks simultaneously.

**RKP_03: The 100-Hour Grace Period — Do the Math Right**
The 91.409(b) grace period allows up to 10 hours over the 100-hour limit solely while en route to a maintenance facility. This is not 10 free bonus hours — it is a safety valve to avoid grounding an aircraft in an area with no mechanics.

The math: subtract every hour of overage from the NEXT 100-hour interval. 
Due at 800.0 Tach. Flew to maintenance, arrived at 807.0 (7 hours over). Inspection completed at 807.0. Next 100-hour due at: 807.0 + (100 - 7) = 807.0 + 93 = 900.0.

Many applicants add the overage instead of subtracting it. Practice this calculation until it is automatic.

**RKP_04: The 43.9 Logbook Entry — Four Required Elements**
When a mechanic or pilot performing preventive maintenance completes work, a logbook entry is required. Under 14 CFR 43.9, the entry must contain ALL FOUR of: description of work done, date of completion, name of the person who did the work, and signature plus certificate number and kind of certificate. If ANY one of these four elements is missing, the logbook entry is legally incomplete and the aircraft has not been properly returned to service.

The registered owner retains records under 91.417. Most inspection records are kept until superseded; AD and major repair records travel with the aircraft forever.

### Key Regulations
- 14 CFR 91.409(a) — Annual inspection (12 calendar months, IA required)
- 14 CFR 91.409(b) — 100-hour (commercial use only, 10-hour grace, penalty subtraction)
- 14 CFR 91.411 — Altimeter/Pitot-Static (24 months, IFR only)
- 14 CFR 91.413 — Transponder (24 months, all required operations)
- 14 CFR 91.207 — ELT (12 months + battery life rules)
- 14 CFR 91.171 — VOR (30 days, IFR only)
- 14 CFR 43.9 — Maintenance recordkeeping (4 required elements)
- 14 CFR 91.417 — Record retention requirements
