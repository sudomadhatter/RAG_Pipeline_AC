# Lesson 27: Primary Flight Controls and Trim Systems
## PPL_PA_I_G_01 | ACS Element: PA.I.G.K1a

### What This Lesson Covers
The aerodynamic mechanism for each of the three primary controls (not just the motion, but WHY), trim function and procedure (establish attitude first, THEN trim), control failure modes (jammed, flutter, disconnected), and the four left-turning tendencies (adverse yaw, P-factor, torque, spiraling slipstream).

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: Primary Controls — Aerodynamic Mechanism**
AILERONS: down aileron increases camber → more lift + more drag (adverse yaw). Up aileron decreases camber → less lift.
Net: one wing rises, the other drops → bank.
ELEVATOR: up elevator downloads the tail → nose pitches UP. Stabilator moves entire horizontal tail.
RUDDER: yaw control — NOT a turning device. Used to coordinate turns, correct crosswind, prevent spin entry.
All three controls are COUPLED — aileron input creates yaw (adverse yaw). Coordinated flight requires simultaneous rudder with aileron.

**RKP_02: Trim — Purpose and Procedure**
PURPOSE: trim relieves control PRESSURE — it does not fly the aircraft or set attitude.
PROCEDURE: (1) Establish desired attitude with primary controls. (2) Stabilize. (3) Apply trim until pressure fully relieved.
NEVER: trim first and release controls — the aircraft will pitch off the trimmed attitude.
Runaway electric trim: (1) Overpower with primary controls. (2) Reduce airspeed. (3) Hit TRIM DISCONNECT. (4) Land.
Types: elevator trim (most aircraft), rudder trim (cruise comp), aileron trim (persistent roll compensation).

**RKP_03: Control Failure Modes**
JAMMED: foreign object, ice in linkage. → Reduce airspeed immediately, assess range of motion, declare emergency if needed, long runway landing.
SLOPPY/SLUGGISH: partial disconnection or structural damage. → Reduce airspeed, evaluate, precautionary landing.
FLUTTER: aerodynamic resonance at high speed → surface destruction in seconds. Prevention: stay below Va in turbulence.
PREFLIGHT: verify full and free movement EVERY flight. Check correct direction — not reversed. Gust locks MUST be removed.

**RKP_04: Four Left-Turning Tendencies**
1. ADVERSE YAW: down aileron → more drag → nose yaws LEFT into lowered wing. Fix: rudder in turn direction.
2. P-FACTOR: high AoA climb → right (descending) blade in denser air → more thrust on right → nose LEFT. Fix: right rudder in climb.
3. TORQUE: engine/prop rotates clockwise → aircraft rolls LEFT. Fix: right aileron/rudder compensation.
4. SPIRALING SLIPSTREAM: prop wash hits left side of vertical fin → yaws LEFT. Fix: right rudder.
All four act simultaneously in a full-power high-AoA climb → right rudder is mandatory.
