# Lesson 28: The Powerplant and Propeller — How They Work and How They Fail
## PPL_PA_I_G_02 | ACS Elements: PA.I.G.K1b, PA.I.G.K1c

### What This Lesson Covers
Fixed-pitch vs. constant-speed propellers (and why the power sequence matters), detonation vs. pre-ignition (two different failure modes requiring different recognition), the dual magneto system and what the runup check actually proves, and engine failure after takeoff — the most critical emergency and why the impossible turn kills pilots.

### The Four Knowledge Points

**RKP_01: Fixed-Pitch vs. Constant-Speed Prop**
Fixed-pitch: blade angle fixed. RPM IS the power indicator. Throttle change → direct RPM change.
Constant-speed (CS): governor adjusts blade pitch to hold selected RPM constant. Power = MP + RPM together.
CS POWER SEQUENCE:
- INCREASING: advance RPM (prop) FIRST, then throttle (MP). High RPM before high power — avoids detonation.
- DECREASING: reduce throttle (MP) FIRST, then reduce RPM. Prevents low RPM at high MP (detonation zone).

**RKP_02: Detonation vs. Pre-Ignition**
DETONATION: fuel auto-ignites from compression heat before the flame front arrives. Multiple ignition points simultaneously.
Causes: wrong fuel octane, lugging (high MP / low RPM), excessively lean, high CHT.
Recognition: rough engine, power loss, high CHT.
Immediate action: reduce MP, enrich mixture, increase airspeed for cooling.
PRE-IGNITION: ignites BEFORE spark plug fires — from carbon deposit or glowing surface.
Both are destructive. Neither is 'the engine exploding' — both are combustion timing failures.

**RKP_03: Dual Magneto System and the Runup Check**
Two INDEPENDENT magnetos: LEFT fires left plugs, RIGHT fires right plugs. Each is complete, self-powered ignition.
Redundancy purpose: engine flies on one mag if the other fails. Dual plugs = better combustion + efficiency.
RUNUP CHECK: 1,700-1,800 RPM:
- Key LEFT → only right mag active → expect 50-125 RPM drop.
- Key RIGHT → only left mag active → similar drop.
- Large or unequal drops = fouled plug or mag issue.
DEAD-CUT TEST: key momentarily to OFF → engine should nearly stop → proves BOTH P-leads are grounded.
HOT MAG FAILURE: if P-lead breaks, mag fires even with key in OFF → propeller is always live → fatal ground-handling hazard.

**RKP_04: Engine Failure After Takeoff — The Impossible Turn**
Immediate action: (1) Pitch to BEST GLIDE immediately. (2) Full mixture/fuel pump/both mags on. (3) Pick landing zone AHEAD.
DO NOT attempt 180° turn back to runway below 800-1,000 feet AGL.
The impossible turn kills: low altitude + low airspeed + bank + reduced stall onset = unrecoverable stall/spin.
Pre-plan the decision altitude: 'Below X feet — land ahead. Above X feet — evaluate the turn.'
The zone straight ahead is always the primary landing target.
