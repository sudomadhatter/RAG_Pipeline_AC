# Lesson 30: The Electrical System — Alternator, Battery, and Electrical Failure
## PPL_PA_I_G_04 | ACS Element: PA.I.G.K1f

**RKP_01: Alternator vs. Battery**
Alternator: engine-driven, primary source, charges battery during operation. Output: ~13.5-14.2V.
Battery: backup source, limited capacity (~17 AH typical). Lasts ~1 hour at full charge with 15A load.
Ammeter CHARGE (+): alternator exceeds load — excess charges battery. Normal in cruise.
Ammeter DISCHARGE (−) in cruise: ALTERNATOR FAILURE. Battery is now sole source.
Response: shed non-essential loads, contact ATC, land before battery depletes.

**RKP_02: Load Shedding After Alternator Failure**
SHED FIRST: landing lights, strobes (day), non-essential avionics (ADF, entertainment), cabin heat.
KEEP: COM radio, transponder (7700), GPS/NAV, pitot heat in IMC/icing.
Calculate: battery time remaining vs. flight time to nearest airport. If battery time < flight time → DECLARE EMERGENCY NOW.

**RKP_03: Electrical Fire in Flight**
Memory items (in order):
1. MASTER SWITCH — OFF (removes all power feeding the fire)
2. AVIONICS MASTER — OFF
3. ALL ELECTRICAL EQUIPMENT — OFF
4. DOES SMOKE STOP? Yes = electrical source confirmed. Ventilate.
5. LAND IMMEDIATELY. Squawk 7700.
CO warning: odorless, colorless — check CO detector; haze + headache + confusion = EXIT HEAT, VENTILATE, LAND.

**RKP_04: Circuit Breakers — Do NOT Reset**
Breaker trips = circuit fault exists. Resetting re-closes the fault → fire risk.
Rule: do NOT reset a tripped circuit breaker in flight.
Exception: if the circuit is SAFETY-CRITICAL and the POH authorizes one attempt — reset ONCE only; do not attempt a second.
Avionics master: prevents voltage spikes from reaching avionics during engine start. Always off during starting sequence.
